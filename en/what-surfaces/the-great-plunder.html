★ What surfaces · Malta, June 2026

The Great Plunder

A five-part series on how Europe is stripping its productive citizens before the lights go out

★ About this series

The Great Plunder is a series of five interconnected pieces written in June 2026 for Het Open Vizier. The series describes the fiscal, demographic and political developments through which Europe is currently driving out its productive class, and uses contemporary South Africa as a mirror for the outcome that awaits us if we continue our current policies.

The series does not take a political position. It describes. The choice of what to do with it lies with the reader.

A mirror for the European voter in the second quarter of 2026. Five texts, one diagnosis, no escape hatch.

The five-part series in five paragraphs

For those who do not have time to read all five parts — here is the core of each part in one breath.

0 · First plunder, then judge

A wage is what someone earns from what they produce. A profit is what is left after everyone has been paid. We have decided that this surplus must be skimmed to finance the bottom and the middle. First we plunder those who feed us. Then we constrain them so that they can no longer keep up with China, America and India. Then we judge them when their factories close. The circle closes itself — and we, the majority, are the executors.

I · Diagnosis — we plunder those who feed us

Five percent of the population carries fifty percent of prosperity. In Germany the DGB demands a one-time wealth levy of ten percent. France and Norway have introduced exit taxes. On 12 February 2026 the Dutch Second Chamber passed a wealth tax on the super-rich by 124 votes to 26. Brussels is working on a European minimum exit tax. At the same time 16,500 millionaires left the United Kingdom. The same pattern unfolds in Italy, Portugal, Spain. We plunder the productive minority faster than they can leave.

II · Mechanics — the walls we have built

Box 3, wealth tax, exit taxes, gift regulations, succession rules. Each reasonable on its own; together a trap from which a family business, an inventor, an entrepreneur can escape with ever greater difficulty. Italy opened a door with the 24-bis flat tax regime (200,000 euros per year, for life) and attracted 5,500 wealthy individuals in two years. Lugano and Ticino do the same. The walls we built to keep the productive in turn out to be gates for those who want out — and prison walls for those who stay.

III · Outcome — South Africa as mirror

Between 2000 and 2020, 100,000 highly skilled people left South Africa. The rand lost 75 percent of its purchasing power. Eskom registered 247 days of load shedding in a single year. Urban water became unreliable. And the plunderers — those who had demanded the redistribution policy — were left with a country that could no longer carry them. The same mechanics now operate in Europe. When the productive leave, it is not the productive who suffer. Those who stay suffer.

IV · The political landscape — no exit within the system

A European left that plunders with moral certainty. A centre-right that bends without knowing how to do otherwise. A populist right that points without building. A Brussels institution that produces documents but not products. Employers' organisations that protest technically but lose morally. The productive class has no political representation left in Europe. Statistically, politically, culturally, morally alone. The way out lies outside Europe. For those who stay, there is no way out anymore.
