Activists Build Continents

Follow-up article · June 2026

Activists Build Continents

Drive seeks ground. Give her ground.

The sun on the equator is half the story. Who harvests it is the other. An open invitation to organizations with vision and to everyone seeking their energy where they can build something.

The thesis of this article

He who has energy but finds no space, destroys. He who has energy and is given ground, builds.

The biomass-equator route from our lead article has technology. What it still lacks is on-site leadership. Not from a Brussels chair. Not from a Hague commission. From the construction site itself. And leadership resides exactly where we rarely look for it now: with the people with too much drive for the European scale.

I · The Chinese Model, Reimagined

China does not send its most difficult people away to punish them. China sends them to villages, to borderlands, to Africa, to Pakistan — not as prisoners but as builders. He who builds something that China trades with strengthens the bond between China and that place. It is the most successful diplomatic export of our century, and it happens without an army.

Europe can do the same. Not the other way around — not as development aid dumping people we no longer see a future for here onto another continent. Rather, as an opportunity. He who gets stuck here and is given space elsewhere to build a life, a business, a community, brings back something no Brussels subsidy can ever buy: an organic connection between continents, carried by people who know both sides from within.

II · What the Equator Needs

The biomass-equator route requires twenty billion euros of trade per year, ten thousand village-sized biorefineries, fifty ports that do not yet exist, and millions of people to support the logistics, chemistry, construction, service, and trade. This is not work we manage from Wageningen. This is work that must emerge on-site, by people living and building on-site.

An equatorial country like Mozambique has talented youth without work. Brazil has engineers without a challenge. Ghana has chemists leaving for London because there is no infrastructure at home. Europe has people with too much ambition for the European pace. Between these four groups lies an industrial revolution waiting to happen. What is missing is a gateway.

III · Three Groups, Three Opportunities

The Pioneers Who Already Paid for Their Crossing

There are currently hundreds of thousands of people in Europe who have paid twenty thousand euros to get here. That is no small sum. That is venture capital, and the willingness to risk it. They are not passive help-seekers. They are the active ones of their generation, who have staked their savings and their risk to change their fate.

And then they find themselves in a European system that cannot utilize their energy. No work permit. No recognition of diplomas. No space for entrepreneurship. The drive that brought them across the sea boils over in an asylum procedure that takes years and a benefit system that demands nothing.

Offer them a different track. Those who want it — voluntarily, with support, with startup capital — can use their entrepreneurship where it matters: a biorefinery in Ghana, a Juncao cooperative in Mozambique, a trading house in Suriname supplying Rotterdam. Not as a return. As an upgrade. With European recognition, European partners, European market access. The twenty thousand euros they invested pays off on a continent where they *are* allowed to build.

The Europeans Who Are Too Cramped Here

There are also Europeans who get stuck here. Not because of birth elsewhere, but because of temperament. The entrepreneur who loses six months in every consultation committee. The engineer who cannot get his idea through the regulations. The activist who makes commercials about what should be and finds no place to do it himself. For them, the same applies: drive seeks ground.

The equator offers ground. Literally: marginal land that doesn't exist in Europe. Figuratively: a terrain without vested interests, without three hundred years of regulation, without a meeting culture that swallows half of every day. He who suffocates here can breathe there.

Organizations with Vision and Networks

And there are organizations. Greenpeace is an organization that has shown in its communication for decades what it wants to see realized: a world where energy is green, where industry respects nature, where the south is not exploited. Its spots are clear, its rhetoric powerful, its networks global.

This is a respectful invitation. The biomass-equator route is exactly what Greenpeace has been advocating for years, translated into industrial form: solar energy where it falls, crops that sequester more carbon than they emit, partnership instead of exploitation, no fossils, no nuclear fission. What has been in campaigns for decades can now be in concrete and in plantations. Greenpeace has the global presence, the trust with equatorial governments, the credibility with the European public, and the organizational capacity to set this up. No other organization has that combination. The invitation is clear and respectful: come build this. Or organizations with the same vision and weight — WWF, 350.org, Fridays for Future if they are old enough to handle coordination — who want to take up the call.

IV · What This Is Not

This is not a plan to dump those who are not welcome here somewhere else. That would be a repetition of what the United Kingdom tried with Rwanda: unloading shame in another country. That does not work and does not deserve to work.

This is something else. This is recognition that the people with the most drive — whether born in Aleppo or in Amsterdam — do not find appropriate space in today's Europe. Their energy gets stuck in procedures, meetings, regulations, and in an expectation of conformity that breaks the very best. A continent that cannot place its most energetic people becomes impoverished. A continent that sends them out into the world to build something that the mother countries then trade with enriches itself twice over.

And it is voluntary. Those who want to stay, stay. Those who want to build where building is allowed, get the path. No exile. No punishment. An opportunity.

V · What It Gains Us Here

A Europe that relaxes its pressure points. Less tension in the neighborhoods where too many people with too much energy have too little perspective. Less radicalization, because radicalization is primarily a consequence of meaninglessness, not ideology. Fewer asylum procedures that take years, because a second track — entrepreneurial, productive, with European support — offers a way out that the current cycle does not know.

And a Europe with trading partners at the equator led by people who understand us — who speak our language, know our rules, can read our markets. Not ambassadors who change every four years. People who have built their lives there, and who are therefore the most natural bridge.

We have always thought that leadership means: falling in line. Our best leaders were precisely the people who fell out of step. We have forgotten that and removed it from our system.

The equator has space for those who fall out of step here. And Europe benefits from what they build there. We lose nothing by letting them go. We gain everything by letting them build.

Greenpeace, take the baton. To everyone with the drive but without the space: look at the sun on the equator. It is there for you.

— Het Open Vizier · June 2026
