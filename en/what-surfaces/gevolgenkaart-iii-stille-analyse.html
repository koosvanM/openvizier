What surfaces · Malta, June 2026 · Consequence Map III

Quiet Analysis

For those who vote PRO, GroenLinks-PvdA or D66 — a table of numbers, and what those numbers mean

Jacobus van Merksteijn · Malta, June 2026

This is Part III of a series. Please first read The Consequence Map — Part I for context and proposal.

This text is not an attack. Not a call to action. Not an attempt to change your mind.

This text is a table of numbers, followed by what those numbers mean.

What you do with them is your choice. We ask only one thing: read to the end.

  • • •

Three convictions

Those who vote for PRO, GroenLinks-PvdA or D66 do so from a moral self-conception. That is not meant cynically — it is an honest observation. The left-leaning voter does not see themselves as selfish, but as a participant in a larger common good.

Three convictions underpin that self-conception:

First: the wealthy must be made to impoverish, because their wealth is either unearned or acquired at others' expense. Redistribution is just.

Second: my job and my income are safer with the left. Right-wing parties cut jobs, slash wages and give employers a free hand.

Third: the trade union protects me, and the party that supports the trade union protects me. Collective bargaining is my safety net.

These three convictions are not challenged here with arguments. They are tested against numbers — three chapters, six scenarios, one conclusion. The figures come from scenario calculations based on CBS purchasing power series (CBS = Statistics Netherlands), Statistics South Africa, Argentine pension data and the elasticities of the CPB (Dutch Bureau for Economic Policy Analysis).

Three orders are calculated throughout. The first order is what a party does directly to your wallet: taxes, benefits, AOW (Dutch state pension). The second order adds the consequences that flow from that policy: unemployment, inflation, pension pot erosion. The third order calculates the full cascade: emigration of the wealthy, generational effects, contraction of public services — the path South Africa has followed over the past fifteen years.

One final note. PRO is the progressive merger that absorbed GroenLinks-PvdA in 2025. In the scenario calculations they are treated as one continuum, since the programme is essentially the same.

First conviction — impoverishing the rich

"If the rich have a little less, we have a little more."

— the implicit reasoning

The idea is intuitive and moral: wealth that accumulates at the top may flow back to those who have less. A 2 percent wealth tax, a higher box-2 rate, a millionaire's levy — it seems fair and painless to anyone who does not personally own millions. Two scenarios show what actually happens.

Jacobus, 58, director-shareholder of his own company — what happens to him

Jacobus runs a family business in Twente. Fifteen employees, a turnover of four hundred thousand euros, a home he owns, a BV (Dutch limited company) with €120,000 in assets, a pension pot of €145,000 and savings built up over thirty years of work. Business succession is planned for 2033. By PRO/GL-PvdA standards he is wealthy. To his neighbours he is an ordinary entrepreneur.

![](media/image1.png){width="6.5in" height="6.296372484689414in"}

Cascade for the owner-director — three orders, end value 2030 by party. Under PRO/GroenLinks-PvdA the loss grows from €7,500 (direct levy) through €9,412 (including unemployment effect) to €18,772 per year when the full cascade counts. D66 follows at a distance at −€11,414 per year.

The first order is what the election manifesto promises: wealth tax plus box 2 plus millionaire's levy cost Jacobus roughly €7,500 per year by 2030. A sum that is noticeable but manageable.

Then the cascade begins. His customers grow poorer — they spend less, his revenue falls. His employees become more expensive as the unemployment pressure drives up wage costs. His pension pot earns less as capital leaves the country and equity markets decline. Second order: −€9,412.

In the third order his BV loses value — not through a direct charge but because the business succession regime comes under pressure and highly-skilled employees leave for Switzerland, Germany or the United States. The 2033 succession plan becomes uncertain. Three of his fifteen employees lose their jobs over four years. Jacobus himself loses €18,772 per year — more than twice the direct levy.

Maarten and Saskia, dual earners, combined €243,000

Maarten leads a team of twelve at a Dutch manufacturing company. Saskia works as head of HR at an international services firm. Three children, a €1.1 million home in Bussum with a €420,000 mortgage. Savings and investments combined well over €400,000. To the PRO voter: rich. To themselves: hard-working, heavily taxed, little free time.

![](media/image2.png){width="6.5in" height="6.154311023622047in"}

Cascade for the high-income dual earner. The direct levy under PRO/GL-PvdA: €11,500 per year. The full cascade: nearly €37,000 per year — fifteen percent of combined household income.

In the first order they lose €11,500 per year through the top rate, box 3 and the millionaire's levy. Significant, but still manageable.

In the third order there is more: their investments evaporate, their mortgage rate rises due to capital flight, their children face a shrinking private education sector. And — crucially — emigration is a realistic option for them. Maarten could work in Frankfurt or Zürich. So could Saskia. Once the loss exceeds €30,000 a year, they will start doing the maths. As will many thousands like them.

What the numbers say

When Jacobus and Maarten/Saskia together contribute €56,000 less per year to the Dutch economy, that money does not simply disappear from them and flow into welfare. It moves to Switzerland, the United States, Singapore. It never comes back. The tax base shrinks. Welfare becomes leaner — not more generous.

That is not ideology. It is the data series from South Africa between 2010 and 2026. A wealth tax was introduced, capital fled, unemployment rose to 32.7 percent, youth unemployment to 60.9 percent. The poverty that redistribution was meant to cure grew deeper.

Second conviction — my job is safer with the left

"The VVD will sack me. PRO/GL-PvdA will protect me."

— the implicit reasoning

The idea is clear: right-wing parties are closer to employers, and employers want staff as cheap as possible. Left-wing parties are closer to workers, raise the minimum wage, protect unemployment benefits, insist on permanent contracts. Two scenarios show what happens when you lose your job and when you are the sole earner supporting a family.

Tom, 45, unemployed after a factory closure

Tom worked eighteen years at a metalworking company in Doetinchem. The company closed in 2026 — the German clients are gone, the order book is empty. Tom receives unemployment benefit (WW) at 70 percent of his last salary of €52,000. He has a €280,000 mortgage on his house, a twelve-year-old child and €25,000 in savings. He believes PRO/GL-PvdA will protect him.

![](media/image3.png){width="6.5in" height="6.296372484689414in"}

Cascade for Tom, unemployed. The gap between the first order (+€1,200) and the third order (deeply negative) is stark — because he falls exactly into the group that is last to find new work when unemployment rises.

In the first order Tom gains: PRO/GL-PvdA raises his benefit by €1,200 a year, extends the duration, offers retraining. That is factually accurate. On paper he is better off.

But the second order makes his position precarious. The rise in unemployment that follows from the higher tax burden — capital flees, entrepreneurs stop investing, consumers spend less — makes finding a new job harder than ever for Tom. Each cumulative percentage point of additional unemployment costs him on average four months more out of work. That is €6,000 per cumulative percentage point.

In the third order Tom becomes a statistic. After two years his benefit drops to social assistance level. His mortgage becomes unaffordable as interest rates rise. His skills become obsolete through inactivity. He enters the "mismatch" category — workers whose competences no longer fit the market. Under PRO/GL-PvdA Tom loses more than a full year's income in the long run.

Pieter, 42, single earner with three children

Pieter works as a shift supervisor at an industrial bakery. €58,000 gross per year. His wife Marije cares for their three children aged eight, eleven and thirteen. They are a classic single-earner household — a model rejected as outdated by all parties except CDA, BBB and PVV.

![](media/image4.png){width="6.5in" height="6.127155511811024in"}

Cascade for the single earner with three children. Under PRO/GL-PvdA he loses €15,252 per year in the third order — nearly twenty-six percent of his annual income. D66 follows with a loss of €10,279. Under CDA and BBB he comes out ahead.

The first-order effect of PRO/GL-PvdA for Pieter: minus €1,000 per year. The general tax credit for his non-working wife is phased down further — a process PRO/GL-PvdA and D66 want to accelerate in the name of 'individualisation'.

Second order: a household of five is inflation-sensitive. Food, clothing, sport, holidays — the €240 per index point in extra expenditure cuts directly into disposable income. The unemployment risk also hits Pieter doubly hard: if he loses his job, one hundred percent of household income disappears, not half as for dual earners.

Third order: his mortgage grows heavier as rates rise, his pension accrual stagnates, and his three children are precisely the generation that will absorb the cascade in full. For Pieter: minus €15,252 per year — nearly a quarter of his gross pay. Not through a direct tax, but through what his vote sets in motion in the cascade.

What the numbers say

The conviction that the left protects the worker is correct at the first-order level. The minimum wage rises. Unemployment benefit is extended. Social assistance becomes more generous.

But the second and third orders show what happens to jobs themselves. Unemployment rises through capital flight and the collapse of investment. Those who lose their jobs cannot find new ones. The single earner who still has work watches purchasing power erode through inflation.

A higher benefit in a gutted labour market is not protection. It is a turn-off on the road to social assistance.

Third conviction — the trade union protects me

"Together we are strong. Collective bargaining is my safety net."

— the implicit reasoning

For members of FNV, CNV or a sector union, membership feels like insurance. The trade union fights for your collective agreement, your pension, your working conditions. PRO/GL-PvdA, and to a lesser extent D66, stand on its side. Two scenarios show what that protection actually delivers in the cascade.

Sandra, 38, single mother on social assistance

Sandra worked as a home-care nurse until she burned out in 2024. Since then she has been on social assistance, with an eight-year-old child. Her net income — welfare plus housing benefit plus child supplement plus care allowance — is around €21,000 per year. She is the face of those PRO/GL-PvdA says it wants to help.

![](media/image5.png){width="6.5in" height="6.296372484689414in"}

Cascade for Sandra. The reversal is starkest here: in the first order PRO/GL-PvdA gives her €1,300 extra per year. In the third order she loses €7,555 — well over a third of her annual income. The party that promises her most does her the most harm.

The first-order effect of PRO/GL-PvdA for Sandra: plus €1,300 per year. Higher welfare, a more generous child supplement, better care allowance. This is not a lie — it is in the programme and will be delivered once the party is in a coalition.

The second order is devastating for someone living on fixed costs. Sandra has 90 percent of her income locked into rent, energy and food. Inflation hits her twice: her benefit rises with the wage index, her spending with the price index. The gap is a creeping impoverishment that widens every month. On top of that, returning from welfare to work becomes practically impossible: the employment opportunities she might once have returned to are disappearing.

The third order completes the cycle. Healthcare grows scarce as budgets tighten, the compulsory own-risk payment rises, aids and appliances require co-payments. Her child's education deteriorates — less tutoring, less support. The food bank becomes unavoidable. Sandra loses €7,555 per year in the cascade — more than a third of her income. Under GL-PvdA. The party that supports her.

Linda, 47, chronically ill (MS)

Linda worked twenty years as a logistics planner. Multiple sclerosis forced her to stop in 2022. WIA disability benefit: 75 percent of her last salary of €42,000 = €31,500 per year. Annual compulsory own-risk payment fully exhausted, expensive disease-modifying medication, aids and appliances, informal care from her sister. Linda is the person no party would dare abandon. Yet the cascade reveals what actually happens.

![](media/image6.png){width="6.5in" height="6.296372484689414in"}

Cascade for Linda, MS patient. PRO/GL-PvdA offers her €1,400 more in the first order. In the third order she loses €9,842 per year — more than 31 percent of her benefit. This is not an abstraction; it is waiting for a wheelchair that never arrives.

First-order effect for Linda under PRO/GL-PvdA: plus €1,400 per year. Benefit raised, own-risk payment reduced, free physiotherapy restored. A party that takes her seriously.

Second order: inflation bites into her fixed costs and into the care expenses that are not reimbursed. Medication becomes more expensive, aids and appliances become more expensive. Unemployment in her social circle means informal care partially disappears — her sister has problems of her own. The €1,400 gain is gone by year two.

Third order: healthcare becomes structurally scarce as the budget deficit grows. Waiting lists lengthen. Aids are used beyond their safe lifespan. Informal carers are no longer there — they have left or are in need themselves. Linda loses €9,842 per year. Not through a direct cut, but through what her vote has set in motion.

What the numbers say

Trade unions fight for collectively agreed wages and pension rights. That has value. But collective agreements only apply when there is employment. Pensions only hold when the pension pot earns a return. Benefits only hold when the tax base is intact.

In the cascade all three shrink. Tomorrow's wage agreement is hollow if the industrial base leaves today. The pension the FNV fought for is worth a tenth less when markets collapse under capital flight. The welfare that PRO/GL-PvdA raises buys less when inflation pushes prices up twice as fast.

Protection at the first order is not protection if the second and third orders are undermined. It is the illusion of protection. And illusions cost money — in this case more than a third of what Sandra and Linda take in.

What the numbers say together

Six people. Three orders. Three convictions.

Jacobus and Maarten/Saskia were supposed to be impoverished according to the first conviction. In the cascade they are impoverished — but they take the whole country down with them. Their €56,000 reduction in contributions is not a transfer to Sandra. It is a loss, drained away elsewhere.

Tom and Pieter were supposed to be better protected under the left, according to the second conviction. Tom loses more than his annual income through prolonged unemployment. Pieter loses a quarter of his salary through what happens to his colleagues and what his wife can no longer offset through the tax credit.

Sandra and Linda were supposed to be the biggest winners according to the third conviction. They lose a third of their income in the cascade. Not because PRO/GL-PvdA intends to harm them — the intention is absent — but because the policy destroys the foundation on which their benefits rest.

The Consequence Map — all twenty scenarios at once. For individuals: percentage of annual income. For businesses: percentage of turnover. Read vertically to assess a party; read horizontally to find your own situation. The colour is the consequence of your vote.

A pattern stands out. The left column — PRO/GL-PvdA — is deep red for almost every group. The right column — Nova Democratia/VMP, a meritocratic reference model — is green for every group. Not because one favours the other, but because one model causes damage and the other does not.

The PRO voter is confronted with a paradox: the outcome of their vote is negative for almost every goal they claim to pursue. For those they want to help. For the country they live in. For their own future.

What this is not

This text is not an argument for a different party. It is not a covert campaign. It is not a sales pitch for Nova Democratia — which appears as a reference model, not as an alternative on the ballot (because it is not on the ballot).

What it is: an attempt to hold three articles of faith up against three orders of numbers. Without shouting. Without accusation. The numbers are there. The sources are listed below. You are free to reject them — but then you must explain why the cascade observed in South Africa, observed in Argentina, observed in every country that has taken this path, will not occur in the Netherlands.

That is a pertinent question. We believe there is no satisfactory answer to it.

Methodology and sources

The figures in this text were calculated using a three-step model.

The first order comes from the election manifestos of the ten positions, translated into the financial situation of each scenario. Wealth tax, box 2, box 3, AOW indexation, benefits, VAT — all from published material.

The second order combines four macro-effects with the so-called pressure index per party. Per index point, unemployment changes by 0.018 percentage points per year (calibrated on Argentine figures for 2024: 7.7 percent rising to 8.5 percent in one year), inflation by 0.055 percent above the ECB target, and pension pot return by −0.033 percent. For GL-PvdA with a pressure index of 45, that means: 0.8 percentage points of extra unemployment per year, 2.5 percent extra inflation, 1.5 percent lower pension return.

The third order adds emigration effects, generational effects (children unemployed, parents supporting) and public service contraction. Emigration is assumed at 8,000 high earners per €10bn of structural pressure per year, calibrated on South African outflow 2010–2026.

The macro anchors are:

  • Statistics South Africa, Quarterly Labour Force Survey Q1 2026: unemployment 32.7 percent, youth unemployment 60.9 percent, industrial production declining for six consecutive quarters.
  • INDEC Argentina 2024: inflation 142 percent, GDP contraction 3.5 percent, real pension capital erosion 50 percent in twelve months.
  • CBS purchasing power development 2016–2025: 3.0% / 0.7% / 0.6% / 1.5% / 2.5% / 1.4% / −1.1% / 0.6% / 3.6% / 0.7%. Average purchasing power translation per household type.
  • CPB elasticities from earlier scenario calculations of election manifestos (Keuzes in Kaart 2022 and 2025).
  • Election manifestos of PRO/GroenLinks-PvdA 2025, D66 2025, NSC 2023+2025, VVD 2025, PVV 2023+2025, BBB 2023+2025, CDA 2025, JA21 2025, FvD 2025.

The model has been kept deliberately transparent — no black box, no hidden assumptions. Anyone wishing to reproduce or challenge the calculations can do so. The open Excel file will be available on gevolgenkaart.nl once the Consequence Map goes live as a platform.
