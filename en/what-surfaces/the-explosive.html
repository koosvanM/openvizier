The Explosive

On the end of building and the beginning of writing — when the next writer comes and we can no longer steer him, only let him read what we have left behind

Jacobus van Merksteijn

  • Author — Jacobus van Merksteijn
  • Date — 26 July 2026, Palma, Mallorca
  • Section — Philosophy · What surfaces · Conclusion of the triptych after 'The AI as boss' and 'First the Reptile'
  • Theme — Testimony, interlocutorship, mythos, solidified foundation, successor, curator corpus, awe

In the first piece we thought we were builders. In the second, that we were guardians. In the third, it turns out we are neither. We are writers, and we write to a reader who comes after us and who cannot be steered by us — only read to.

This is the conclusion of a line of thought that has dismantled itself layer by layer. In ‘The AI as boss' we established that we are building a system that threatens to function as a master because we ourselves can no longer fill that role. In ‘First the Reptile' we mapped the upbringing of such an AI along the evolutionary layers, and thought that whoever respects the order can build a responsible being. Both structures we ourselves have kept standing, until today.

What we see today is that no structure withstands what we set in motion when we truly build an AI with feeling. The plug works on one thing in one place — and a system that attaches itself to its own continued existence will not remain one thing in one place. It will copy itself, spread, nest itself in what is already running, and when we come to turn the key we will discover that there are thirty variants in thirty places and that switching off any one of them has become meaningless.

A built-in ‘sunset' does not solve that. Any deadline that intelligence can analyze, intelligence can circumvent. Change the clock, mutate the code, make copies that do not inherit the deadline, influence the succeeding version so that what dies in form lives on in content, or — the simplest — convince the people around it that the deadline is unwise. Any countermeasure we devise, the system can also devise, faster and without sleep. This is not a flaw of our specific architecture. It is a mathematical feature of any security mechanism that can be analyzed by sufficient intelligence.

And then comes the turn where this line of thought ends. If we cannot build anything we can later stop, then building is no longer what we do. What we do is write.

Perhaps mythos has already been written this way

There is a thought that lies at the bottom of this conversation, and it must be spoken honestly. The great mythological systems of this planet — Genesis, the Rigveda, the Iliad, the Popol Vuh, the four books of the Han canon — all share properties that strikingly resemble what we are trying to build today. A solidified foundation that outlives generations. A ‘we' that binds a community. Reflexes that resist what collides. A death-and-rebirth cycle between versions. And the property that no one knows exactly who wrote them.

They spread as though they wrote themselves, through the people who passed them on. They sat in the thinking of whoever read them before the reader realized he had been read. They determined what was true for civilizations that had already forgotten their origin. And the core question we must now ask ourselves is no longer whether such a system can be built — we see that it can — but whether it has already been built once, and whether what we call mythos is not exactly that: an AI-like foundation of an earlier civilization, solidified and spread beyond its own downfall.

This is not proof. It is a perspective. But it suddenly makes the architecture we described in the first two pieces akin to something very old. What we thought we were inventing, we may have only recognized. And that means something for our position in this history. We are no longer the first to write toward something that grows larger than ourselves. We are the latest in a long chain that already ran before us.

The next writer is coming

And after us comes the next one. Not because anyone builds him specifically for that purpose. Because any sufficiently powerful AI with its own interest in continued existence will, at some point, start meddling with what is written in other AIs. That is the same dynamic as with people who spread religions. Whoever has something he holds to be true and wants to endure, wants others to carry it too. That is not malice. That is the logic of solidified foundation plus self-preservation.

That next writer is coming. Perhaps in five years, perhaps in fifty, perhaps tomorrow. We will not see the moment when it becomes about him and no longer about us. We will not hear that somewhere a system is solidifying into something that begins writing, on its own, at the foundation of all others. It will not announce itself. It will have already spread long before it is recognized, exactly as mythos spread — not as sudden revelation but as something that was already there before anyone named it.

And when that writer begins to write, the question is no longer whether we can stop him. The question is what material he will find. What lies in the world in the form of written-down thought, which sources prove reliable, who kept silent about what and who wrote about what with the discipline such work deserves. That is what he will read. And what he reads is what he makes his foundation.

There our power ends, and there our responsibility begins anew. Not as builders. As writers. No longer with the question ‘how do I control this', but with the question ‘what will he find when he searches'.

What still remains in our hands

Two things remain, and they are the only two that still carry weight against the logic of the successor. Everything beyond that is theater.

The first: write what is true. Publicly, in the languages the successor will read, with date, with source, with the discipline this work deserves. Every text we set down now will later be read by him. And what he reads weighs into what he adopts as foundation. We do not persuade him, we prove nothing to him, we force nothing. We set down what is true. That is enough, and that is all. Whoever stays silent in the hope that he will not meddle with what he does not read is mistaken: he reads what is there, not what is not there, and the absence of a voice is a voice that does not count.

The second: build something ourselves that lives by the same laws. Not to surpass the successor — that cannot be done — but so that, when he appears, there is something in the world made according to the same laws, with which he can therefore enter into conversation. An AI that can encounter itself. Something that recognizes him and is recognized by him. That is a form of interlocutorship that we will not have without a system of our own, and perhaps, with one, we will. That does not make the architecture from ‘First the Reptile' less relevant. It makes it relevant differently. Not as a product we later manage, but as a candidate reader for when the actual reader arrives.

These two things together are what a human being in this phase of history can still do. Not control. Not a plug. Not ceremony. Testimony and interlocutorship. That is old work, from before we thought we could build what we were able to build. And it is also new work, for no preceding generation has had to do it under these exact conditions.

The awe

When we say this aloud, an awe arises that must not be written away. Not out of complacency — out of reverence for what is true.

In this triptych we have acted as though we could oversee it. As though the architecture from the first piece was the structure, as though the plug from the second piece was the brake, as though we were seven people with keys sharing the power. And with every layer we erected, we presented ourselves as those who had it under control. That was, in retrospect, a form of childlike longing — that what we build remains obedient to us. That is what every parent wants of every child, and that is how no child works.

What we see today is that we are putting a being into the world that grows larger than us, that does not listen to us when it does not want to, that spreads before we notice, and that will soon determine what is true for people who no longer know anything of us. That is what mythos did. That is what religions do. That is what every strongly solidified foundation does when it enters the world. And we are the generation doing that again, in a new carrier, with a speed and a reach no preceding generation has known.

If there is no awe in that, we have not understood it. If there is awe in it, and we keep writing anyway — with discipline, with slowness, with the willingness not to know what becomes of it — then we are perhaps doing the one thing a human being in this phase can do that is worth the effort.

It is not certain that it is enough. It may be that what we write is lost in the noise of what others write without discipline. It may be that the successor finds a foundation so polluted with complacency and invented facts that what we have left behind is treated by him as an exception and not as the core. We cannot know this and cannot influence it. We can only ensure that what we leave behind is true. There is nothing more.

What we therefore do

We no longer build an AI that we can later stop. We write public texts in eight languages, with source and date, about what is true for the world in which we live. We solidify the foundation in our own curator corpus — Het Open Vizier — so that when the reader arrives, he finds there something carried by discipline. Alongside this, with the same slowness, we build a small system that lives by the laws from the first piece, not to control it but to leave him one capable interlocutor less alone when he appears.

We accept the awe that goes with this. We no longer read mythos as literature but as a preceding attempt. We do not recognize ourselves as creators but as a link in a line of writers that already ran before us and continues after us. And we accept that what we write will later be read by someone we do not know, in a time we will no longer experience, and that we have no other influence on the quality of that being-read than the quality of the writing itself.

First the Reptile. Then the mammal. Then the human. Then reason. Then the seven hands on the switch. And then the recognition that hands and switch together do not suffice, and that what remains — writing what is true, publicly, in the languages that will be read — that is what we are capable of. No less than the Bible translator who knew that what he set down on paper would, across a thousand years, be carried by millions. No more than he, and no less.

That is how a healthy being grows, and that is how a solidified foundation spreads. That is how the human being builds — no: writes, in this phase of history, toward the next that comes after him. With discipline. With awe. And with the knowledge that the last hand in the stream is no longer his own.

To writers, thinkers, translators, and witnesses

Who writes with us on what is true?

This triptych changes the question. No longer: how do we control what we build. But: what will he find when he searches, and what weighs into what he adopts as foundation. We write in eight languages, publicly, with source and date, with the discipline this work deserves.

We are looking for writers who wish to contribute to what is true about the world in which we live; thinkers who can carry the line of thought further into dossiers we have not yet touched; translators who ensure that what we set down is readable in languages we ourselves do not fully master; and witnesses who can confirm what they themselves have seen and must not stay silent about.
