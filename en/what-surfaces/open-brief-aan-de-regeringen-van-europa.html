Open letter to the governments of Europe

\n

To the heads of government, ministers, parliamentarians, and policymakers of Europe,

\n\n

With this letter, I put one question to you. A question that Europe has been avoiding for years. A question that, in 2026, can no longer wait.

\n\n

Why don't we start today, on a large scale, with Carbon-Alert BiCRS in combination with ethanol-SOFC?

\n\n
\n

We have the unemployment. We have the infrastructure. We have the technology. We have the learning curve. We have the feedstock. We have the factories that can convert solar panels and wind turbines.

\n

What is missing is not money. What is missing is a political decision that does not lean on subsidies, but on market figures that are lying on the table.

\n
\n\n

\n\n

Europe has everything it needs — today

\n\n

1. The unemployed are waiting for meaningful work

\n\n

Spain counts 2,6 million unemployed. Italy 2,0 million. France 2,4 million. Germany 2,8 million. The Netherlands 380.000. A large part of them has technical training or is practically skilled.

\n\n

The Carbon-Alert chain needs personnel at exactly those levels: pellet production, distillation operations, SOFC installation, maintenance teams, BECCS-CO₂ logistics. Permanent, non-outsourceable jobs. Spread across all regions. Not Silicon Valley elite-work — industrial labor that Europe excels at.

\n\n

2. The infrastructure is already there

\n\n
    \n
  • Gas stations: 120.000 in Europe, mechanically identical for gasoline, diesel and ethanol — only the label changes
    • \n
  • Distilleries: already present in every grain, beet and wine region
    • \n
  • Pellet factories: 1.200+ EU factories that can directly supply cellulose feedstock
    • \n
  • Gas pipeline network: 70.000 km existing, usable for regional ethanol distribution or biogas co-transport
    • \n
  • Empty industrial sites: former coal, refining and automotive sites — direct locations for BiCRS+SOFC hubs
    • \n
\n\n

3. The technology is proven

\n\n

No prototype, no proof-of-concept, no "still needs to scale up". Nissan has been running a 70-percent-efficiency ethanol-SOFC on a trial scale in Tochigi since 2026. Ceres Power is delivering 50 MW SOFC stacks to Doosan this year, plus the production license to Weichai. IPEN/Nissan/VW/Stellantis have been working together on an ethanol fuel cell platform since 2017. Lawrence Berkeley proves that an HEA catalyst can work with 80 percent less precious metal. At the same time, Bosch discontinued its SOFC branch and Stellantis stopped with hydrogen — not because the technology fails, but because they did not move fast enough.

\n\n

Why Carbon-Alert asks for no subsidies

\n\n
\n

Every subsidized technology carries a political expiration date. SDE++ can pay €100 per ton of CO₂ today, €50 tomorrow, nothing the day after tomorrow. Business plans that rely on subsidies perish when regulations change.

\n

Carbon-Alert is deliberately designed to be profitable even without any subsidy — not because we reject support, but because we want to be independent of political whims.

\n
\n\n

\n

Understand the difference. Without subsidy, a hub pays for itself in 3 years. With SDE++, in 1 year. Both figures are solid. But the real story is that our technology stands on its own. That is the crucial message to every government: you don't have to give money — you just have to not get in the way.

\n
\n\n

Why delay is irresponsible

\n\n

The window is closing within three to five years. Doosan is building 50 MW SOFC capacity in Korea. Weichai is building a factory in China. Bloom Energy dominates the US. If Europe does not act now, all large-scale SOFC supply chains will be Asian or American within five years — and Europe will be stuck with the same mistake as with solar panels (China), batteries (Korea/China) and chips (Taiwan/US).

\n\n

This is not a warning. This is a recurrence for which we can prepare this time.

\n\n
\n\n
\n

What we ask

\n

Five decisions that cost no money and change everything

\n
    \n
  1. Permit acceleration — for BiCRS factories and SOFC hubs within 6 months instead of 2 to 3 years.
    2. \n
  2. Standardization of ethanol gas station — European norm for E100 conversion of gasoline pumps. Costs nothing. Works tomorrow.
    3. \n
  3. Policy neutrality — no EV monopoly in zero-emission classification. Ethanol-SOFC must also count.
    4. \n
  4. Education modules — vocational training for ethanol operation and SOFC maintenance in 500 ROCs and MBOs.
    5. \n
  5. Procurement priority — public buildings, hospitals, data centers may choose Carbon-Alert hubs without formal technology neutrality blockade.
    6. \n
\n
\n\n
\n\n

None of these measures cost the treasury anything. They only clear away the political undergrowth that blocks healthy market development today.

\n\n

The hidden bill of the current path

\n\n

Those who choose the battery EV and the green hydrogen strategy implicitly choose enormous externalities that are not yet on the price tag, but will have to be paid later.

\n\n
\n\n
\n

Carbon-Alert has none of these externalities. The pellets come from forest and agricultural waste streams. The CO₂ is recovered (BECCS net-negative). The infrastructure is there. The jobs are created in regions where unemployment is highest. And the price per kWh, per km, per MWh is right — without the taxpayer stepping in.

\n
\n\n

Finally — a personal word

\n\n

I am not a lobbyist and not a consultant. I am inventor, entrepreneur and publisher. For years I have worked on the technology, the patents and the economic models that lie before you. I am not asking for money. I am asking for attention, space and speed.

\n\n

It is naive to think that a European government in 2026–2027 still has time for the wrong technological choices. It is equally naive to think that the market will solve everything itself — while bureaucracy and political preference slow down the learning curve.

\n\n

What I ask for is a fair chance for a technology that stands on market figures, not on subsidies — a technology that works, is affordable, provides jobs, recovers CO₂ and makes Europe more independent.

\n\n
\n

Start today. Not with money — but with the willingness to give Carbon-Alert space.

\n

In three years, the market will point to the winner. It is up to you to determine if that is Europe — or another continent that has acted more wisely.

\n
\n\n

Sincerely, and in sincere expectation of your response,
