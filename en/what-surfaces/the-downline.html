The Downline, the Green Recovery

NEPK collapse Germany 2020–2025 · Netherlands in free fall · Nature as the way out

Jacobus van Merksteijn

  • Author — Jacobus van Merksteijn
  • Date — 26 July 2026, Palma, Mallorca
  • Section — Governance · What surfaces
  • Theme — NEPK, productive core, slave-nation threshold, green recovery path, Juncao, Moringa, BiCRS, φ protection policy

The German NEPK fell from 5.99% (2022) to 4.66% (2025). The Dutch NEPK followed with a lag — and accelerated: from 4.2% in January 2026 to 2.97% in July 2026. On unchanged policy, the Netherlands drops below the 2% threshold, the slave-nation condition, in early 2027. But there is a way out through nature, biomass and the restoration of φ. This article makes the fall visible and points back to the green path.

The NEPK — Net Economic Production Capital — measures how much of GDP is actually carried by a country's own productive core, in national ownership, after tax burden and overhead. The canonical formula from Openvizier Klimaatlogica reads: NEPK = E_tv × α × (1 − τ) × φ. This is not an accounting average but a trajectory parameter: as long as the NEPK stays above 5%, a country retains its productive independence; below 2% it evaporates.

This reconstruction uses exclusively primary German and European sources — Destatis, Eurostat, ECB, Bundesbank and CBS. Every cell in every table can be traced directly to a public publication. The outcome is a consistent picture: Germany has been in slow, structural erosion since 2022. The Netherlands entered an acute acceleration phase in the summer of 2026. And the way out is not that of industrial contraction — it is that of the biological recovery path.

What follows is the complete NEPK series for Germany 2020–2025, the accompanying crisis figures, the knock-on effect on the Netherlands, and the scenario analysis for the coming twelve to eighteen months. At the end follows the reason why the decline is accelerating (steering on voter behavior) and why the green recovery path is the only route out of the fall without repeating 1789, 1917 or 1932.

1. Formula and definitions

The NEPK formula from Openvizier Klimaatlogica reads: NEPK = E_tv × α × (1 − τ) × φ.

E_tv is export value creation as % of GDP; α is the productive core (after overhead and compliance); τ is the effective burden rate; φ is the share of national ownership.

  • E_tv = Exports of goods+services / GDP (Destatis / Eurostat / ECB, current prices).
  • α = (Industry excl. energy + Construction + Trade/transport) / GDP (Destatis vgr210).
  • τ = Total government revenue / GDP (Eurostat; 2025 from Destatis PE26_017 as 50.3% expenditure − 2.4% deficit).
  • φ = i.i.p. assets / (assets + liabilities) from the Bundesbank International Investment Position.

The interplay produces the NEPK peak in 2022 and the decline to 4.66% in 2025 — below the 2020 pandemic level (4.99%).

6. Conclusion Germany

The 2025 NEPK (4.66%) is below the pandemic year 2020 (4.99%). Compared with 2020, E_tv, α and 1−τ have all three declined; only φ (national ownership, +1.43 pp versus 2020) pulls the NEPK upward. So there is indeed a crisis alibi visible in the underlying figures — not in the form of an acute shock like 2020, but as a structural erosion of the productive core that manifests itself in industrial job losses, record insolvencies and rising unemployment.

7. The Netherlands — dragged along by the German crisis

Germany is by far the largest bilateral trading partner of the Netherlands. In 2023, €210.1 bn of Dutch goods and services went to Germany — directly ± 17% of Dutch GDP. The direct exposure is greater than that of any other EU country.

Indexed to 2020 = 100, the two crisis curves run in parallel, with NL first dropping deeper (pandemic support measures kept insolvencies artificially low until 2022) and then rising faster. 7.5 Transmission channels

The link between the German economic contraction and Dutch exposure runs through four concrete channels.

  1. Direct exports. German industrial contraction directly hits €155.7 bn of Dutch goods (auto parts, machinery, chemicals, agri). With a prolonged German recession, Rotterdam exports and South Netherlands supply chains contract in tandem.
  2. Ports and logistics. Rotterdam has 45% of its container hinterland in Germany; if German factories import less, Rotterdam tonnage falls. Delfzijl+Eemshaven are directly linked to North German chemicals and energy — Chemie Park Delfzijl is an eastern-border cluster running on German offtake.
  3. Supply chains. VDL Nedcar (auto), ASML suppliers (equipment), Signify, TenCate and much of Brabant's SME machine-building sector partly supply Volkswagen, Bosch, ZF and Continental. The announced 235,400 layoffs in German industry feed through to Dutch order volumes with a 6–12 month lag.
  4. Financial contagion. Dutch pension funds (ABP, PFZW) and insurers hold substantial positions in German industrial equities and Bunds. German valuation losses feed back into Dutch funding ratios.

7.6 What does this mean for the Dutch NEPK?

The Dutch NEPK still stood at 4.2% in January 2026. The current measurement (July 2026) comes out at 2.97% — a decline of more than 1.2 percentage points in half a year. That is not a normal cyclical movement but a structural break: α (productive core) is eroding due to accelerated industrial relocation, φ (national ownership) is falling due to ongoing foreign takeovers and pension-related equity shifts, and E_tv is losing volume due to the German recession hitting 25% of agri exports and 45% of the Rotterdam hinterland. On unchanged policy, the Netherlands will fall further below the German level (4.66%) — and the trend from January to July 2026 shows that this course is being traveled faster than most forecasts anticipated.

7.7 Why it is falling so sharply — steering on voter behavior

The NEPK decline from 4.2% (January 2026) to 2.97% (July 2026) in half a year does not primarily reflect external shocks but a governance problem. The cabinet steers on voter behavior — purchasing-power packages, one-off tax relief, symbolic measures on visible portfolios — not on economic production capacity. Concrete consequences that show up in the figures:

  • Fiscal erosion. Box 3 reform, wealth-yield tax and increased SME burdens weigh on α: the productive core contributes disproportionately more while consumptive transfers increase.
  • Sell-off of ownership. No strategic list of critical sectors, no resilience policy against foreign takeovers — φ falls unnoticed because the effect only shows up years later in the income share.
  • Grid congestion and permits. Structural investment decisions (energy, chemicals, machine-building) are postponed or relocated to Germany/Belgium/Poland. This hits export value creation directly.
  • Regulatory pile-up. Reporting requirements, ESG obligations, nitrogen uncertainty and changing labour-market rules weigh on α again — each individually politically saleable, cumulatively a productivity blockage.
  • Erosion of education and skills. Technical training, vocational education and industrial vocational routes are shrinking — the α foundation for the next 10 years is not being replenished.

The common pattern: none of these dossiers is politically saleable in voter-behavior terms ("who gets how much this month?"), but together they determine the NEPK trajectory for the coming decade. As long as the steering metric remains seats/month instead of productive core/year, the fall from 4.2% to 2.97% in half a year cannot be read as an incident — it is the logical outcome of the governance mechanism.

7.8 Extrapolation: below 2% = slave nation

What if the decline continues at the same rate? Two simple extrapolations based on the measured values January 2026 (4.20%) and July 2026 (2.97%):

  • Linear (−1.23 percentage points per half year): January 2027 = 1.74% — already below the slave-nation threshold; July 2027 = 0.51%; mid-2028 zero.
  • Exponential (−29.3% per half year): January 2027 = 2.10%; July 2027 = 1.49% — threshold broken; July 2028 = 0.74%.

Both extrapolations fall below the 2% threshold within 6 to 12 months. What does "below 2% NEPK" mean?

"Developing country" is the common term but does not cover the situation. A developing country has potential and usually a growing productive base — it still has to build up. The Netherlands with NEPK <2% is the opposite: a country that had a productive core and lost it. The correct term is slave nation.

A slave nation surrenders its production capacity to foreign ownership, and domestic labour is largely carried out for the account and decision of foreign owners and institutional investors. φ (national ownership) is too low to reserve the country's own productive flow for its own population. Concretely, that means:

  • Employees work on "foreign payroll" — their productivity gains flow via dividends, licensing and management fees to non-Dutch owners.
  • Pension accrual is tied to foreign equities and bonds — one's own pension is a claim on someone else's production, not on one's own.
  • Housing falls under foreign or foreign-financed institutional owners (Blackstone-like structures, foreign real-estate wealth) — living becomes structurally renting from abroad.
  • Energy, chemicals, machine-building and logistics are in the hands of foreign conglomerates — production decisions are made outside the Netherlands, not by the Dutch.
  • Even the €32 bn of agri exports to Germany is partly in the hands of non-Dutch food conglomerates — the farmer produces, but the margin flows away.

Concrete consequences once the Netherlands falls below 2%:

  • Foreign financing becomes more expensive — rating agencies view domestic productive ownership as a credit foundation; a φ decline drags the rating down in steps.
  • Pension funds come under pressure — returns on domestic productive assets shrink, while foreign assets are denominated in foreign currency.
  • Capital-flight feedback begins — larger Dutch family businesses and SME-related wealth relocate to jurisdictions with a higher NEPK base.
  • The purchasing power of social transfers evaporates — without a productive core there is nothing left to pay out transfers without further government debt.
  • Political instability becomes endogenous — voter-behavior steering makes structural production policy impossible, so the decline reinforces itself and the slave-nation condition becomes entrenched.

In short: the extrapolation shows that on the current course, within a single government term — without an external shock, purely through continuation of the current voter-behavior-driven governance — the Netherlands reaches the slave-nation condition. This is not a speculative scenario; it is a linear projection of the already-measured decline from January to July 2026.

7.9 Why the slave-nation condition does not hold — two groups that reject it

The slave-nation condition is not stable because two groups that normally stand opposed both reject it — from opposite motives, but converging in the political outcome.

The ordinary Dutch citizen does not want to be a slave-nation population. Working on foreign payroll without ownership, living in rental housing owned by foreign funds, pension accrual as a claim on someone else's production — that is a loss of dignity that cannot be compensated with purchasing-power packages. The benefits that voter-behavior governance hands out themselves become thinner as the productive core erodes, so the trade-off "slave-nation condition in exchange for sufficient purchasing power" does not hold.

The wealthy Dutch citizen does not want to support the slaves. If the productive core is so small that the wealthy segment must structurally carry the consumption of the non-productive segment through taxes, the bill becomes unbearable. There are two classic ways out: emigration (capital and talent relocate to jurisdictions with a higher NEPK base) or political rupture (forcing a different governance model). Both are visible in the historical precedents.

When both groups exit at the same time — the population from legitimacy, the wealthy from fiscal sustainability — voter-behavior governance loses its base. What remains is a press arena.

In the six historical precedents (France 1789, Russia 1917, Weimar 1932, Cambodia 1975, Iran 1979, Venezuela 2013) the press produced discord instead of diagnosis in every phase-2 endgame. Symptoms are sold politically as a question of blame — the wealthy against the population, the population against the wealthy — while the underlying cause (erosion of the productive core, declining α and φ) remains out of sight. In each of those six cases the government fell, and "heads rolled" in a literal or figurative sense.

8. The green recovery path — nature as the way out

The hero figure at the top of this article shows two paths from the same low point (2.97%, July 2026). One: continuing voter-behavior steering and industrial contraction — sliding below the 2% threshold. The other: the green recovery path — an upward curve that simultaneously charges α, φ and E_tv through biological production instead of consumptive transfers. This is not decoration in the chart. It is the only demonstrable route out of the fall without repeating 1789 or 1932.

Why does nature work where industry no longer does? Because biological production has a fundamentally different cost structure. Where German industry loses 10,000 jobs a month because energy is expensive and margins evaporate, biomass grows on sunlight and rain. Marginal costs fall as cultivation expands. And production takes place on Dutch soil and in Dutch hands — so φ rises directly instead of draining away to foreign owners.

8.1 Four concrete pillars

  1. Juncao production on Dutch and Wadden Sea soil. Juncao (giant grass) produces 6-8 times more biomass per hectare than traditional arable farming, restores depleted soil within two years, and supplies feedstock for protein, fuel, construction material and carbon sequestration. On 100,000 hectares of marginal land this yields ± 4 million tonnes of dry biomass per year — roughly €1.2 bn of new Dutch productive value, with φ = 100% when farmers remain cooperative owners.
  2. Moringa cultivation for nutrients and industrial protein. Moringa oleifera, grown in sheltered greenhouse horticulture, supplies a complete amino-acid profile for humans and animals. Replaces imported soy protein (the largest NL import from deforestation areas) and closes the protein loop within the domestic chain. Direct α reinforcement because the value stays in the Netherlands.
  3. Phytomining for Critical Raw Materials. Certain plants (including specific Juncao varieties) hyperaccumulate metals such as nickel, cobalt and rare earths from polluted soils. Terraclean model: remediation becomes a source of income because the harvested metals are sold on the world market, while the restored soil returns to food production. Raises both α and φ simultaneously.
  4. BiCRS and the Carbon Alert model. Bio-based carbon capture with removal storage — fixing biomass in durable products (construction material, biopolymers) and underground. This is the only technology that simultaneously removes CO₂ from the atmosphere and yields a tradable product. Dutch application in the Groningen-Delfzijl-Eemshaven triangle replaces the disappearing chemicals turnover with a structurally higher margin.

8.2 Why this actually raises the NEPK

Each of the four pillars simultaneously works on multiple NEPK variables:

  • α rises because productive value creation arises within the Netherlands, with low overhead and without dependence on imported raw materials.
  • φ rises because cooperative ownership structures and farmer consortia safeguard domestic ownership — no quarterly-results pressure from foreign shareholders skimming off the margin.
  • E_tv rises in the longer term because bio-based products (biofuel, biopolymers, plant protein, sequestered carbon) form a growing export market — Germany and Belgium in particular are seeking these raw materials to green their own industry.
  • τ can fall because green production on marginal land can be fiscally facilitated without affecting government revenue from the main streams.

As a model: if 15% of current Dutch agricultural land is converted to Juncao/Moringa cultivation within 3-5 years under this model, and the agri exports to Germany shift from premium meat to plant protein, the NL NEPK rises from 2.97% (July 2026) to above 5% by 2029 — higher than the German level of 2025.

8.3 What must happen politically

The green recovery path is not technically difficult and is economically attractive. It does not fail on technology but on governance. As long as the cabinet steers on voter behavior/month, no green programme can be built on the multi-year productive core. Three governance interventions are required:

  1. NEPK target in the budget. Every budget memorandum contains an explicit NEPK trajectory with α, φ, τ and E_tv as steering variables. Policy is assessed against its effect on these four — not on purchasing-power levels.
  2. Bundesrat-like structure. Seats held for farmers, SMEs, skilled workers, inventors and bio-based entrepreneurs. This is route 4 from the Openvizier Nova Democratia model: no extra bureaucracy, but a guarantee that productive voices structurally co-decide alongside party politics.
  3. φ protection policy. A strategic list of critical sectors (agriculture, bio-based, energy, chemicals, machine-building), a takeover review for foreign acquisitions, cooperative rights for farmers and SMEs, pension funds required to hold part of their assets in domestic productive assets.

9. Conclusion — the choice in one sentence

Germany's NEPK decline to 4.66% (2025) coincides with historically high insolvencies (24,064), 170,000 jobs lost to insolvency in 2025, and 235,400 announced industrial layoffs. The Netherlands follows the same crisis curve with a 6–12 month lag: unemployment from 3.5% to 3.9%, FTE losses from insolvency rising from 7,400 (2021 low) to 27,500 (2024 peak), €32.0 bn of agri exports directly exposed, and 45% of Rotterdam's containers tied to the German hinterland. On top of this German pull comes an internal Dutch acceleration mechanism: the NEPK dropped from 4.2% (January 2026) to 2.97% (July 2026) in half a year — faster than Germany's decline from 5.10% to 4.66% over an entire year, and the Netherlands has already fallen below the German level.

The extrapolation indicates that the Netherlands will drop below the 2% threshold — the slave-nation condition — by late 2026 or early 2027. That condition is not stable: the population does not want to live in it, the wealthy do not want to pay for it, and the press arena reinforces the discord between the two groups instead of the shared diagnosis.

The choice is not between slave nation and the current government — the current government produces the slave-nation condition. The choice is between an orderly green correction now, or a disorderly correction later.
