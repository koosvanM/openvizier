The Five Development Axes

Core implementation — sub-report for policy formation. From solutions to calculations.

By Jacobus van Merksteijn

Reading guide

This sub-report contains three parts, in the order that gives the reader the fastest picture of what can be gained:

PART I — The solutions presents the seven scenarios and shows which scenario produces which result. Anyone who only wants to know “what must be done?” need read only this part.

PART II — Graphical projections shows the seven projection charts which demonstrate, for each outcome (PISA, WIA, burnout, etc.), how each scenario develops through 2075. Calculated using a neural influence model.

PART III — Calculations explains which inputs (causes) and outputs (outcomes) are included in the model, which sources were used, which weights and delays were fitted from the data, and with what reliability (R²). Anyone wishing to verify the calculations should read this part.

The full report (v28, 107 charts) contains the complete substantiation for each subtheme.

PART I — The solutions

1. Seven intervention scenarios

2. Core conclusion

Only the Dutch Renaissance improves all outcomes simultaneously toward the target value. The Short-circuit protects S1 (childcare, attachment) alone but has no effect on PISA reading. The Media Brake protects screen time/iPad use but not labor-market outcomes. Every combination of partial measures leaves gaps. The calculations show that only an approach addressing all five inputs simultaneously can reverse the four loss episodes.

3. Recommended course

3.1 Immediate steps (2026–2028)

  • Mandatory €50/month subscription levy per connection for all suppliers operating in the Netherlands (Q3 2026 — see 3.2)
  • Talent-retention package: tax incentives for top incomes (Q4 2026)
  • Migration selection based on knowledge and skills (Q1 2027)
  • AI redeployment program for displaced administrative staff (Q2 2027)
  • Parenting reform ages 0–7: abolish childcare, facilitate stay-at-home parent (Q3 2027)
  • Establish Nature boarding schools ages 7–18 (Q4 2027)
  • Craft renaissance + danger-and-boundary program in the curriculum (Q1 2028)
  • Reindustrialization + energy autonomy + AI production capacity (Q2 2028)

3.2 Subscription levy as a policy instrument

The levy addresses the two media inputs (screen time and iPad-at-30-cm) through market mechanisms at supplier level. Enforcement is limited to a few dozen providers rather than 17 million citizens, making the measure feasible within the Dutch enforcement context.

Design of the levy:

  • Rate: €50 per month per active connection (SIM, eSIM, primary account). Applies to everyone, with no age limit.
  • Scope: Every provider of mobile connectivity or platform services supplying the Netherlands (telecom, internet service providers, platform operators).
  • Enforcement: Provider collects and remits monthly. Non-compliance is penalized with a multiple of the unpaid remittance, escalating to market bans for repeated violations.
  • Allocation: 50–75% flows back into the treasury. This relieves the Dutch population through tax reductions (payroll tax or VAT). The remaining 25–50% goes to the bridging fund for parenting reform (Nature boarding schools, stay-at-home parent support, redeployment programs).

Expected revenue and effect:

  • Volume: Approximately 20 million active connections × €50/month × 12 = €12 billion/year gross revenue.
  • Redistribution: 50% treasury share = €6 billion/year in tax reduction → approximately 3% lower payroll tax per worker.
  • Behavioral effect: Higher costs discourage unnecessary second and third connections. Parents receive a price incentive not to give children their own connection — without a ban, through market mechanisms.
  • Transmission: In the influence model: reduction in screen time and iPad-at-30-cm through price elasticity. Expected effect: screen time 7.8 → 5–6 hours/day; iPad-at-30-cm 120 → 60–80 min/day among ages 0–7.

3.3 Steady-state costs and benefits

The Dutch Renaissance requires €60–65 billion per year in structural investment (steady state from 2050). Cumulatively, it yields +€1,480 billion through 2075. Break-even is reached in 2050. The levy additionally generates €12 billion/year gross, of which 50–75% flows back to the treasury as tax reduction (€6–9 billion/year).

3.4 Feasibility and risk

With political support above 35%, the Dutch Renaissance is the best scenario. With support between 25–35%, the Future-oriented parenting chain (+€820 billion) is a good second choice. With support below 25%, the Short-circuit (+€635 billion) and €50 Levy (+€260 billion) remain defensive options because they reach break-even quickly (2029–2030) and are politically straightforward to introduce.

The following seven charts show how each scenario affects each measured outcome through 2075. Calculated with a neural influence model that reproduces the measured data from 1970–2025 with R² between 0.75 and 1.00. For each outcome, the chart gives the target value and the 2075 end value per scenario in the legend.

P.1 PISA reading literacy among 14-year-olds

Target 505 (pre-2015 level). The Dutch Renaissance reaches 475 in 2075 — 30 points below target. Integrated parenting chain 461, €50 Levy alone 454. Short-circuit and Nature boarding schools alone have no effect on PISA reading because they do not address the determinants (screen time, teacher shortage, iPad-at-30-cm). Crucial insight: interventions must target the right inputs.

P.2 PISA mathematics among 14-year-olds

Target 530. The Dutch Renaissance is the only scenario that produces significant improvement. The decline in mathematics requires addressing several inputs simultaneously: teacher shortage, screen time, lack of outdoor play and iPad-at-30-cm.

P.3 PIRLS reading literacy, ages 7–14 cohort

Target 550. PIRLS measures 10-year-olds, so it provides a signal 4–7 years earlier than PISA. The Dutch Renaissance rises toward 550; the Integrated parenting chain reaches 540.

P.4 Burnout among workers aged 21–35

Target 12%. Childcare → burnout has Δ=20 years. Interventions beginning in 2027 show an effect from 2047. The Dutch Renaissance and Integrated parenting chain decline to 12–14% by 2075.

P.5 WIA inflow per 1,000 workers aged 14–35

Target 8. The most dramatic improvement is possible: from 30 to 7–8. A sharp bend appears around 2047 due to the Δ=19-year delay. Outcome: €20–25 billion per year in savings for the healthcare system.

P.6 Adults aged 21–35 living at home

Target 10%. The Dutch Renaissance and Short-circuit reach approximately 10–12%. Childcare and maternal employment are dominant determinants.

P.7 Classical myopia as an indicator of sensory blindness

Target 30%. The Dutch Renaissance is the only scenario that significantly reduces it (to 30–40%). iPad-at-30-cm and lack of outdoor play are dominant. Outcome: STEM programs receive entrants with better 3D ability.

7.1 Interpretation of R² values

  • R² > 0.9 — Very good fit. Model reproduces the data almost completely.
  • R² 0.7–0.9 — Good fit with some unexplained variance.
  • R² < 0.7 — Weak fit (for the myopia cohort, due to only 6 measurement points).

11. Limitations and outstanding points

  • Weak fit: Myopia cohort R²=0.49 due to only 6 measurement points over 100 years.
  • Projection uncertainty: Projections through 2075 are model outputs, not predictions. Orders of magnitude ±20–30%.
  • Model assumption: The model treats biological delays as fixed; interventions do not change them.
  • Exogenous: Exogenous shocks (climate, war, epidemics) are not included in the model.
  • Political: Political feasibility is not modeled.
