Malta, 19 June 2026 · Lead article · Current affairs

The anti-immune disease of our government strikes

The nitrogen package late June 2026 — an attack on the last productive tissue. And there is another way.

Jacobus van Merksteijn · 19 June 2026

What is on the table this week:

Zones of 500 to 1,000 meters around twenty large Natura 2000 areas. Within these, 2,850+ livestock farms around the Veluwe alone must disappear, relocate, or innovate. Reduction: 65%, ambition 69%.

Source: WNL, 17 June 2026

National reduction 42-46% for agriculture and horticulture in 2035. Industry and mobility: 50%. Company-specific emission standards, forced land-bound dairy farming by 2032, continuation of Lbv buyouts.

Source: Taskforce Landbouw, Natuur & Stikstof, 28 May 2026

€212,795 per kilo of nitrogen is what the Lbv buyout already costs. A kilo of nitrogen in concentrated feed costs €1.50. 140,000 times more expensive than the problem. To achieve the 2035 government goals via buyouts: €106 billion.

Source: ESB, 16 June 2026

Agricultural added value –20% in 2026 alone. Barns are being dismantled and rebuilt in Poland, Sweden, Croatia, Peru, and Ukraine. Specifically the young, modern barns.

Sources: Rabobank, 12 June 2026 · NieuwRechts, 15 June 2026

That is the package. What it actually is, in the language of medicine:

The anti-immune disease of our government, which we previously described in this newspaper, is now striking in full force. The leadership attacks the productive tissue based on 2019 calculation models, in a 2026 climate, to protect habitats that are already shifting northwards themselves.

The madness in a single figure

One indicator summarizes the entire illness.

€212,795. That is what the State pays to remove one kilogram of nitrogen from a Natura 2000 area via the Lbv buyout scheme.

That same kilogram in concentrated feed costs the farmer €1.50.

The societal damage of that kilo, highest estimate: €8.80.

So we are spending €212,795 to prevent €8.80 in damage. A ratio of 1 to 24,000. No thinking person can defend such a decision. And yet the policy is continued — and even expanded.

Why? Because the system no longer thinks. It executes. The Raad van State said in 2019: nitrogen must come down. The models say: this much per year. The bureaucrats say: therefore, this many farmers gone. No one asks anymore: is this entire construct actually correct?

What nature is doing in the meantime

While we calculate based on 2019 goals, reality is moving.

The tree line is shifting northwards. Mediterranean species are establishing themselves in Brabant. Scandinavian coniferous forests are shrinking, birds are nesting weeks earlier, insects are shifting their distribution hundreds of kilometers per decade.

The habitats for which we are buying out farmers are moving out of their protected areas themselves.

We are protecting postage stamps of vegetation types for which the climate no longer provides. We are fighting for the existence of species in places where they no longer want to be. And we are doing so by buying out the farmers who could support them — through nature-inclusive agriculture, circular farming, soil carbon.

That is not policy. That is an auto-immune reaction to a reality that the body no longer perceives.

Two scenarios

There are exactly two paths for the next ten years. One is the package of Friday, June 26. The other is what healthy leadership would do.

Ten indicators. Two outcomes. The choice is on the table this week.

Scenario A — The cabinet package

The system carries the anti-immune disease through to the end.

  • 5,000 to 8,000 livestock farms disappear
  • Agricultural value shrinks by 40 to 60% over ten years
  • Barns relocate en masse to foreign countries — where livestock herds grow
  • Global nitrogen emissions remain the same or increase
  • Costs to the State: €25 to €106 billion
  • Permitting lock remains a problem (PBL: nitrogen remains a pressure factor)
  • Nature does not recover sufficiently — desiccation and acidification remain untouched
  • Climate shift is not taken into account

A patient growing weaker under increasingly heavy medication. A country attacking its own productive cells to protect habitats that won't be there tomorrow.

Scenario B — The other way

Three principles. No more attack on the productive tissue.

One. Tax what you want to reduce, instead of chasing away what you need. ESB research points the way: a nitrogen excise duty of €2.20 to €8.80 per kilo on concentrated feed and artificial fertilizer. No expropriation of complete companies — a targeted incentive that allows farmers to choose extensification, circularity, or continuing as is. Revenue: €1 to €3 billion per year. For the State, a net gain instead of a net cost.

Two. Stop with zoning and buyouts. Terminate Lbv and Lbv-plus immediately. Withdraw the 500- and 1,000-meter zones. Replace the legal basis (KDW as a legal standard) with a regional objective that moves with climate shift. The cabinet is already working on replacing KDW with emission targets per sector — that is an opening that must now be filled radically.

Three. Accept that habitats shift. Natura 2000 dates from 1992. The Habitats Directive assumes static areas. But habitats move with the climate. The Nature Plan that the Netherlands must submit to Brussels by 1 September 2026 can anchor this new principle: protect moving habitats, not immutable postage stamps of outdated vegetation.

The living proof that it can be done

In Friesland, a cooperative of farmers works under the name Agricycling. They process urban waste streams into compost and replace artificial fertilizer with it.

In 2025, they processed 12,600 tons of waste streams into 11,340 tons of compost. In doing so, they replaced 317,940 kg of artificial fertilizer — which corresponds to 85,844 kg of nitrogen. No buyout, no subsidy of €212,795 per kilo. A working circularity with a net societal value of €4.4 million per year.

Source: Duurzaam Ondernemen, 27 May 2026

One cooperative. A handful of farmers. Scalable to the whole of the Netherlands. But it doesn't fit into the cabinet package — because it requires farmers who stay, and the package is built on the idea that farmers must go.

Scenario A: €212,795 per kg less nitrogen, via buyout.

Scenario B: 85,000 kg of nitrogen per year replaced via circularity, with revenue.

Both work. One kills the farmer. One strengthens him.

What science has known for a long time

The most painful part of this situation: there is no longer a scientific debate.

  • ESB (16 June 2026): buyout doesn't work; taxing and auctioning do
  • Professor Erisman (House of Representatives, 2026): a 25% national reduction suffices if approached selectively — not the 42-46% the cabinet demands
  • PBL (Nieuwe Oogst, 21 May 2026): nitrogen reduction alone does not lead to nature restoration — desiccation and acidification are equally large pressure factors
  • Agricycling Friesland: circular agriculture provides concrete proof that there is another way
  • Wageningen UR: buffer strips have a 5 to 10% effect on nitrogen in water, but only after 1-5 years — generic policy will not meet the goals

All the same signal. And the leadership ignores it all. Not out of malice. Because it cannot recognize the sickness.

What must be done now

The five decisions of the first hundred days

  1. Withdraw the June 26 nitrogen package. Do not adjust it, withdraw it. It is fundamentally wrong.
  2. Stop Lbv and Lbv-plus immediately. Dismantle the buyout circuit. €212,795 per kg is not policy.
  3. Introduce a nitrogen excise duty. €2.20-€8.80 per kg on concentrated feed and artificial fertilizer. Revenue to nature-inclusive transition and circularity pilots.
  4. Abolish KDW as a legal standard. Replace it with emission targets per sector, linked to a moving habitat concept.
  5. Rewrite the September 1, 2026 Nature Plan. Protect moving habitats. Acknowledge climate shift as a given. Stop with postage stamps from 1992.

The end of self-deception

The cabinet package of Friday, June 26, will be presented as "balanced", "scientifically grounded", and "necessary". It will be supported by GroenLinks-PvdA, D66, VVD, CDA, NSC, and largely by the employers' organizations and FNV.

It will be presented as the solution.

It is the sickness. The anti-immune disease of our government, which is now entering its most aggressive phase.

And at exactly that moment, the citizen must say: not this. Not with our names. Not with our votes. Not with our barns, our land, our future.

Another package is ready. It costs nothing. It yields returns. It lets the farmer live. It lets nature breathe. It accepts the climate. It only lacks one thing: a leadership that can think.

Until it arrives, the responsibility lies with us. With the farmer who won't leave, with the managing director who still believes, with the SME owner who sees it coming, with the employee who feels it on their paycheck.

We are the Treg cells. We are the healthy counterforce that can sideline the autoimmune cell line — but only if we arouse ourselves.

June 26 is coming. The package is coming. It is time to refuse it. And to put the other one on the table.
