The Mechanics of Tipping

From 5,000 to 5 million readers — how a line of thought changes Europe

Jacobus van Merksteijn · Malta, June 2026

The sixth piece in the Consequences Map series. Five Consequences Maps are in place — the diagnosis is complete. But diagnosis changes nothing. This piece offers the mechanics: how mass movements arise historically, and what Het Open Vizier can concretely do to set that staircase in motion.

The six-step staircase

From 5,000 direct readers (diagnosis) to 5 million reached plus political adoption — six steps, each with its own ingredients. Reach estimates are model-based, not a guarantee of success.

The three-element formula

Diagnosis × Vocabulary × Multiplier = Tipping point. Together, the three ingredients form the necessary condition. None is sufficient on its own — all three together offer no guarantee, but they do constitute the only demonstrable condition.

Why the masses are not listening now — three reasons

One — The pain is spread out (€200 a month less); the blame is concentrated (looking for a single enemy).

Two — The vocabulary has been occupied by both populists (“Brussels is to blame”, “the elite”) and technocrats (“it’s nuanced”).

Three — The feedback loop is slow: the political consequence comes four years later, while the experience is daily.

The full explanation

Five Consequences Maps stand. The Dutch, the German, the Maltese, the Brussels, and the Trump mirror. Twenty scenarios each, three orders deep, four countries wide. The diagnosis is complete.

But diagnosis changes nothing. A cancer patient is not cured by an MRI scan. What remains is the most difficult question: how does one take this line of thought from a publication platform with a few thousand readers to the European mass audience of tens of millions?

• • •

Why the mass audience is not listening now

Before considering how to reach the mass audience, one must honestly understand why it is not being reached now. Three reasons operate simultaneously.

First reason --- the pain is dispersed, the blame is concentrated

An Italian pensioner loses 6.7 per cent of his purchasing power. In model terms, this is a cascade of Pillar Two, EU sovereign yields, Trump tariffs and pension-fund erosion. But what he feels is €200 less per month in his account. The pain is real, tangible and daily. The causality is dispersed, abstract and invisible.

Those who do not see the causality vote for whoever shouts the loudest --- usually the populist who points to a single enemy (Brussels, migrants, the elite). A meritocratic diagnosis that says ‘it is all four at once’ is experienced by the ordinary voter as intellectual excuse-making. Not because he is stupid, but because he has no time to weigh four causes simultaneously.

Second reason --- the vocabulary is occupied

European political language moves along five axes: left-right, pro-EU/anti-EU, climate-industry, open-closed, and old-new. All five axes are already occupied by established parties. Anyone wishing to stand between those axes --- Trump’s objectives without his means --- has no word that the ordinary voter immediately recognises.

Macron solved this with ‘ni gauche ni droite’ (neither left nor right). Meloni with ‘sovranità + nazione’. Wilders with ‘eigen volk eerst, ongekuist gezegd’ (our own people first, put bluntly). All three forged a phraseology that could be summed up in two words. Without that word, every movement remains a think tank.

Third reason --- the media architecture punishes nuance

A three-order cascade analysis with twenty scenarios and ten instruments does not make it onto TikTok. Nor the front page. Nor the NOS Eight O’Clock News. The formats that reach the mass audience are designed for one-sentence emotions: a tweet, a 60-second video, a newspaper headline. Anyone trying to fit nuance into them loses by default.

This is not a new problem. The Enlightenment did not win through Voltaire’s Encyclopédie but through his pamphlets. Marxism reached the masses not through Das Kapital but through the Communist Manifesto. Anyone wishing to reach the mass audience must always write two texts: the rigorous one, and the marketable one.

• • •

The three-element formula

Historical mass movements reveal a pattern. None of them turns on arguments alone. None of them turns on a single ingredient. They all turn on three elements simultaneously, in combination.

The three-element formula --- event × word × vehicle = tipping point. One missing: no tipping point.

Element one --- the event

An unmistakable shock that confirms the diagnosis. A shock that fits in a newspaper, a news item, a WhatsApp message among family. Not a trend, not a report, not a warning --- an event.

Macron had his event in January 2017: Le Canard enchaîné published that François Fillon, the favourite candidate of the centre-right, had paid his wife Penelope a fictitious parliamentary assistant’s salary for years. The Penelopegate affair destroyed Fillon within four weeks; the centre-right voter had to go somewhere; Macron was ready. Without that scandal, En Marche would probably have achieved 12–15 per cent --- not the presidency.

Meloni had her event between 2021 and 2022: the cost-of-living crisis hit Italy harder than most EU countries because of its energy dependence. Inflation above 8 per cent, gas bills that doubled, bread 30 per cent more expensive. Fratelli d’Italia had had the luxury of remaining in opposition since 2018; when the crisis came, it was the only uncontaminated right-wing voice. From 4.3 per cent (2018) to 26 per cent (2022) --- without that crisis, the rise would probably have stopped at around 12.

Wilders had his event in November 2023: a combination of the Ter Apel asylum centre becoming overwhelmed, a VVD leader bringing down the Rutte IV cabinet over migration, and a strong performance in the final-week debate. On 15 November, the PVV was still polling at 17 seats; on 22 November, it won 37. Seven days to a tipping point.

What is the event for the meritocratic centre? In model terms, it is unavoidable by 2027–2028: a major BASF departure announcement (already partly under way), a German recession visibly affecting the Mittelstand, or an Italian spread crisis in which ECB intervention becomes politically untenable. It will come; the only question is when.

Element two --- the word

A recognisable vocabulary without populist baggage. Not an ideology, not a programme --- a word. A phrase that can be understood in two seconds and carries the entire diagnosis.

Macron’s ‘ni gauche ni droite’ --- three words, endlessly repeatable, excluding left and right simultaneously without insulting either. Meloni’s ‘Dio, Patria, Famiglia’ (God, Fatherland, Family) --- three words from a fascist past, recognisable to the disappointed conservative without pointing to that past itself. Wilders’ ‘eigen volk eerst’ --- four words that can formally always be denied (‘I meant it differently’), but which convey the message unmistakably.

Het Open Vizier already has the word package. ‘Consequences Map’ (concrete, unpopulist, neutral). ‘Quiet analysis’ (self-classification as anti-noise). ‘Trump as mirror’ (clever --- mentioning Trump without being pro-Trump). ‘Take over the objectives without the means’ (the exact position between copying and ignoring). ‘Meritocracy without populism’ (a combination no existing party dares to claim). The word element is in place.

Element three --- the vehicle

A credible person or coalition that gives the words flesh. Not a think tank, not a platform, not an academic --- a face that can appear on a chat show, a name at the bottom of a party list, a biography that makes the words credible.

This is where the meritocratic centre is lacking. Het Open Vizier has an author, a platform and a methodology. But it has no Macron, no Meloni, no Wilders. It has a line of thought without a face.

This can be resolved in two ways. One --- by building a political vehicle oneself (Nova Democratia as an actual party). Two --- by offering the manifesto to existing centrist parties looking for substance: Volt at EU level, some CDU factions, Forum factions in the Netherlands, Italy’s Azione, and remnants of Spain’s Ciudadanos. The author of the Consequences Maps can decide whether to become the vehicle or serve one. Both are legitimate; both require different investments.

Three historical tipping curves

Three precedents show three different tipping speeds. None resembles the others. None is reproducible as a template. But together they demonstrate the mechanism.

Macron --- 13 months from founding to presidency. Meloni --- 9 years from founding to premiership. Wilders --- 7 days from polling obscurity to largest party.

Macron --- the event creates the vehicle

Macron founded En Marche in April 2016 in his home town of Amiens. He had no party structure, no elected office, no mandate. What he did have was a word (‘ni gauche ni droite’), a biography (former investment banker and former Hollande minister), and a network (Bayrou, moderate PS, moderate LR). For a year, he stagnated at around 15 per cent in the polls.

Then came the event. Penelopegate broke in January 2017; Fillon’s credibility collapsed. Bayrou’s endorsement on 22 February 2017 strengthened the centre. Hamon’s Socialist nomination paralysed the left. Within four weeks --- January to March 2017 --- Macron rose from 18 to 26 per cent in the polls. From there, the mathematics of the second round were predictable: against Le Pen, Macron gathered the anti-Le Pen bloc and won with 66 per cent.

Lesson: without an event, Macron would not have become president. But without years of preparation for the event --- founding a party, crafting a word, building a network --- the event would have left no one to whom voters could defect. Macron was the driest fuse in a wet election.

Meloni --- perseverance rather than an event

Meloni grew more slowly but more durably. Fratelli d’Italia was founded in 2012, received 2 per cent in 2013, 4.3 per cent in 2018 and 6.5 per cent in the 2019 European elections. No sudden rise --- a slow build through perseverance.

Her strategic choice was to remain in opposition when Lega and Forza Italia did participate in the Conte and Draghi cabinets. On the face of it, that looked like a defeat --- in reality, it was an investment. When the cost-of-living crisis came in 2022, she was the only right-wing party not held responsible. Lega and Forza Italia had supported the Draghi government; Meloni had said no. Between 2021 and 2022, Fratelli d’Italia tripled in size.

Lesson: the tipping point need not be exogenous (like Penelopegate). It can be endogenous --- a long build-up in which one chooses the right position for the foreseeable crisis. For the meritocratic centre, this means choosing its position now for the crisis coming in 2027–2028.

Wilders --- the final-week shift

Wilders is the rare case of a complete election surprise. On 15 November 2023, the PVV was polling at 17 seats --- exactly what it had won in 2021. On 22 November, it won 37. In seven days, it increased its position by 118 per cent.

Three factors worked together. One: VVD leader Yesilgöz said in a debate that PVV leadership could no longer be ruled out --- which effectively became a recommendation. Two: the Ter Apel asylum centre dominated the final week. Three: Wilders had the sharpest closing statement, while the moderate alternatives (NSC, BBB) lost credibility in the final week.

Read: the mass can shift in days. Not in a year, not in a month --- in days. For the meritocratic centre, that means the material must be ready before the final week of the relevant election. A European Parliament election in 2029, a German election in 2029, an Italian one possibly sooner. Whoever does not have their stockpile ready will miss the shift.

The six-step ladder

In theory, the three-element formula is complete. In practice, it does not explain how to move from five thousand readers to five million. That is what the six-step ladder is for. Each step multiplies reach by approximately ten, requires its own resources, and cannot be skipped.

*Six steps, each an order of magnitude in reach. Steps 1–2 are under your own control; 3–4 require alliances; 5–6 require luck and a stockpile.*

Step 1 --- complete the canon (Q3 2026)

Five Consequences Maps are in place. Two are missing: a French and a Polish one. That completes coverage of the full European political spectrum --- from Macron’s France to PiS’s Poland, with Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, Malta and the EU in between.

Why complete it? Because a French voter must be able to recognise themselves in it, and so must a Polish voter. Without those two, the project remains north-west European. With them, it covers all five political families in the EU: Renaissance/Renew, the EPP, S&D, ECR/PfE, and the meritocratic exception. Reach after step 1: approximately 5,000 direct readers via openvizier.org.

Step 2 --- the one-minute formats (Q4 2026)

Each Consequences Map gets three derivatives: one infographic (Twitter, Facebook, LinkedIn), one 60-second video (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube Shorts), and one 800-word essay (Substack, Medium, newspaper opinion piece). The long-form text remains for those who want the detail; the short formats spread it.

This is not translation --- it is reformulation. An infographic is not a condensed article; it is a visual argument that stands on its own. A 60-second video is not a summary; it is a different medium with different rules. An 800-word essay is not an excerpt; it is an opinion piece with one central proposition.

Seven Consequences Maps × three formats = 21 derivative artefacts. Reach after step 2: approximately 50,000 readers/viewers via social media and guest publications.

Step 3 --- a coalition of publishers (Q1–Q2 2027)

Not you alone. Not one author writing about seven countries. You seek out four to six European authors --- a German economist, a French entrepreneur, a Czech industrial leader, an Italian journalist, a Polish policy adviser --- who adopt your model for their own country and audience. Het Open Vizier then becomes a hub, not a monologue.

The Excel model promised in every article (‘will be made available at gevolgenkaart.nl when the platform goes live’) is precisely the gateway. Give it away. Open source. Anyone who wants to model their own country can do so. Anyone who wants to improve the model can do that too. The methodology is the weapon, not the secret.

Reach after step 3: approximately 250,000 readers, spread across six countries, with a dedicated author for each country.

Step 4 --- a political vehicle (Q3 2027)

Up to this point, everything has been publishing. Now comes the choice. Does Nova Democratia become a party, or does it become a manifesto adopted by existing parties?

Arguments for founding a party: full control over the message and candidates, no dilution, faster decision-making, and a possible Macron trajectory. Arguments against: enormous financial and organisational investment, a high risk of remaining marginal at 2–3 per cent without winning a seat, and a longer road to political power.

Arguments for offering the manifesto to existing parties: they already have the infrastructure (a pool of candidates, membership, funding), your material can reach a large audience rapidly, and you retain the intellectual property. Arguments against: dilution is inevitable, parties adapt it to their existing support base, and there is no guarantee that they will adopt the core ideas.

A third option merits consideration: a hybrid. Offer the manifesto to two or three smaller meritocratic parties (Volt EU, Italy’s Azione, some wings of the German CDU), while Het Open Vizier itself remains the platform. Reach after step 4: approximately 1 million people familiar with the Nova Democratia framework.

Step 5 --- harness the event (2027–2028)

When BASF partially relocates to Texas --- something that has already begun in 2025–2026 and will be unmistakable by 2028 --- your material must be ready. Ready to go. Not written in a panic afterwards. In a crisis, people look for explanations that already exist, not for new stories.

The same applies to other predictable events: a German recession by 2027, an Italian sovereign-spread crisis when the ECB tightens rates, a major escalation in a trade war. Building up a stockpile is the strategy here --- not improvising when the moment arrives.

Concretely: every Consequences Map must have a ‘crisis version’ of the one-minute format, ready for publication within 24 hours of the event. A BASF-departure-specific infographic waiting for the announcement. An Italian-spread scenario video ready to go. Material in the drawer, to be published when the moment arrives.

Reach after step 5: approximately 3 million people, because the event itself is public and your material comes to the fore as an explanation.

Step 6 --- institutional embedding (2028+)

The final step is reached when a think tank, a university, or a Member of the European Parliament incorporates your model into a report, motion, or curriculum. It then moves from blog to source. From publication to citation.

Concrete candidates: the Bruegel Institute in Brussels, the Centre for European Reform in London, the Jacques Delors Institute in Paris/Berlin, the Clingendael Institute in The Hague, and ISPI in Milan. These institutions seek fresh analysis, but rarely build new models themselves any more. A well-presented Consequences Map methodology based on open data is more attractive to them than one might think.

Reach after step 6: approximately 5 million people directly, and --- more importantly --- an institutional footprint that sustains the framework beyond the electoral moment. Policymakers, civil servants and journalists will use the vocabulary without mentioning its origin. That is the real shift.

What history does not tell us here

To be honest, this ladder carries three risks about which the three precedents do not provide sufficient insight.

Risk one --- the vehicle fails

Macron became president, but Macronism as a movement is dead. La République En Marche won 308 seats in 2017, lost its absolute majority in 2022, and stood at a marginal 7 per cent in the polls in 2026. The vehicle proved insufficiently durable to carry the movement.

What this means for Nova Democratia: one face is not enough. The manifesto must be more durable than any individual vehicle. Methodology + open data + a coalition of authors is far stronger than one person --- even if it takes longer to build.

Risk two --- the event does not happen (in time)

The model predicts a crisis by 2027–2028. But models are more often wrong than right. It is possible that Europe will muddle through without an unmistakable event --- simply declining slowly, without a Lehman moment, a Penelopegate, or a Ter Apel.

In that scenario, the Meloni route remains: perseverance. Remain in opposition for a long time, deliver a consistent message, and be ready when the event eventually arrives. Not all movements shift in the same period; some take fifteen years.

Risk three --- a populist hijacks the message

The greatest risk is that an AfD, PVV, Rassemblement National or Vox picks up your Consequences Map figures and combines them with populist means. The diagnosis is adopted; the method is not. The result is a nationalist using your analysis as evidence for an anti-EU conclusion, while ignoring your meritocratic alternative.

This cannot be prevented. Open data is open. Your protection lies in two places: (1) the meritocratic framework itself --- ‘Trump’s goals without his methods’ cannot structurally be appropriated by populists without self-denial, because their brand is precisely their method; (2) the vehicle --- a credible meritocratic face can claim the message, whereas a populist can only steal the figures.

What this means for Het Open Vizier

The question was: how do you get the masses on board? The answer is no secret, no magic, no brilliant idea. The answer is: build the three elements, climb the six steps, and wait for the event.

For the author of the Consequences Map series, today --- 16 June 2026 --- five points are relevant:

One --- complete Parts VII and VIII (the French and Polish Consequences Maps) in Q3 2026. This completes step 1 and covers the European spectrum.

Two --- begin the communications kit (seven pieces × three formats = 21 artefacts) in Q4 2026. This is the first lever: moving from thousands of readers to tens of thousands through reformulation alone.

Three --- actively seek European co-authors in Q1 2027. A German, French, Italian and Polish publishing counterpart who will adopt the model for their country. Do not ask them whether they would like to contribute to your pieces; ask them whether they would like to build their own Consequences Map using your model.

Four --- make the Excel model publicly available in Q2 2027. Not as a marketing tool, but as infrastructure. Open data drastically lowers the barrier to institutional embedding (step 6).

Five --- settle the Nova Democratia question by Q3 2027 at the latest: party, manifesto offer, or hybrid. Without that decision, step 4 cannot begin.

This is a four-to-six-year project. A shift by 2029–2030, not 2027. Macron did it in thirteen months because he encountered an exceptionally favourable convergence of circumstances. Meloni did it in nine years through consistent perseverance. Wilders did it in seven days because his stockpile was twenty years old. None of the three is your template; your template lies somewhere between Meloni’s perseverance and Macron’s readiness for the event.

• • •

Conclusion --- the quiet catalyst

Those who climb this ladder do not see others climbing it. The populists climb more loudly, the established parties climb more complacently, and the academics do not climb at all. Quiet analysis finds its audience not by shouting, but by being in the right place at the right time.

An Italian pensioner will never understand a three-step model with twenty scenarios. But after an Italian spread crisis, he will understand that someone warned him months earlier. That someone had the figures ready for what happened. That someone proposed a different path, one not founded on populism. Then he shifts—not towards the loudest voice, but towards the right voice.

Whether that succeeds depends not only on Het Open Vizier. It depends on history, on the event, on the messenger, on chance. What lies within your control is the reserve. Build the reserve—six rungs, three elements, four to six years—and wait.

*"Those who wish to convince the masses do not write for the masses. They

write for the moment when the masses begin to search. That moment will come; your

work must already be there."*

— The Mechanics of Shifts, concluding paragraph
