The Plant Route

Why energy via the plant world is the only route that can actually reduce CO₂ emissions — and why it is the skeleton to which the other routes attach wherever they fit.

By Jacobus van Merksteijn

Integrated full-LCA analysis of bioethanol, methanol, Micro-CHP, SOFC and root storage compared with solar panels, offshore wind, electrification, green hydrogen and battery EVs. Metric: gCO₂eq per functional unit — including EOL removal, residual foundations, soil compaction, land use, grid reinforcement, copper/aluminium mining, materials bottlenecks, maintenance, failure sensitivity and supplier logistics.

Guiding principle. CO₂ reduction is the reference value for every form of energy generation on Earth. All comparisons are primarily based on gCO₂eq per functional unit.

What “full LCA” means in v7. All emissions across the complete life cycle, including items official figures leave out: mining destruction; actual production location; charging and grid losses; end of life (thermoset blades, offshore foundations); soil compaction; food competition; embodied CO₂ from grid reinforcement; copper and aluminium mining; the complete materials bottleneck (Ir/Pt/Nd/Dy/Li/Co); and NEW in v7: operational-phase emissions (O&M, failure sensitivity with replacement emissions, service vessels offshore wind 2.3–4 t CO₂e/GWh) and the complete logistics chain (marine freight for batteries 4.1 kg CO₂e/kWh; blade escorts; installer kilometres).

Definition of CO₂ reduction. Counted: (a) avoided fossil emissions and (b) permanent biogenic sequestration for 100–200 years. Not counted: BECCS from fermentation, afforestation, or credits without physical storage.

“Every community generates its own clean energy from local biomass — in the way life itself has been doing it for billions of years.” — Jacobus van Merksteijn, Carbon-Alert

Part III — Transport

What the ICCT figures leave out

ICCT 2025 EU Passenger Cars suggests 63 gCO₂/km for BEVs. Nine omitted items:

  • Grid mix: Actual EU mix Q1 2025: 242 g/kWh (Ember), not 150.
  • Losses: 17 kWh/100 km → 21 kWh after losses.
  • Chinese battery: ICCT 2,750 kg CO₂ per pack (EU); actual China-dominant figure 4,500–6,000.
  • EOL: Less than 40% of EV batteries recycled. EOL gap 200–500 kg/pack.
  • Grid reinforcement: ~2 Gt CO₂ embodied in the grid for 100M BEVs.
  • Copper: 85 kg Cu × 4.1 t CO₂/t = 348 kg = 1.7 g/km.
  • Aluminium: 250 kg Al × 15 t/t (China) = 3,750 kg = 18.8 g/km.
  • Marine freight for batteries: 4.1 kg CO₂e/kWh (Transport & Environment/Kim 2019). Per 75 kWh: 308 kg = 1.5 g/km allocated to logistics.
  • Failures + replacements: Out-of-warranty BEV battery replacement $5–16k; 1–9% failure rate over 15 years; proportionally allocated ~2 g/km.

What a heat pump + grid can and cannot handle

New construction with perfect insulation on a green grid: heat pumps are excellent. 80% of existing EU homes: COP collapse on the coldest days. Plus a 2–4% failure rate per year (Green Reach 2026) means 50–100M replacement events among 100M heat pumps over 25 years. Each heat-pump compressor contains 15 kg of copper and ~2 kg of R-410a refrigerant (GWP 2088) — one heat-pump leak equals ~4 t CO₂ equivalent.

Part VI — The materials bottleneck of electrification

IEA reality: 50% supply gap in 2030

IEA Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2024: “expected supply meets only half of projected lithium and cobalt requirements and 80% of copper needs by 2030”. Columbia Energy Policy 2023: lithium shortage of 300–768 kt LCE in 2030. Asian Development Bank 2024: lithium, copper and nickel fall short.

PEM electrolyser iridium, confirmed three times

IRENA 2020: current Ir/Pt production 3–7.5 GW/yr; 100 GW/yr needed in 2030. German Hydrogen Council 2023: 27 t Ir needed in 2030, production 7–9 t/yr. US DOE 2022: “highly constrained by available supply of Ir”.

EU calculation: 40 GW PEM × 40% share × 350 kg/GW = 5.6 t iridium = 75% of global production for one EU rollout in 2030. EU alone in 2050: 72 t = ten years of total production.

China’s rare-earth monopoly

IEA 2025: China accounts for 60% of mining, 91% of refining and 94% of magnets. Concordia 2026: “refining monopoly is the real chokepoint”. China’s Ministry of Commerce, April 2025: export restrictions on Sm/Dy/Tb.

Part VII — Maintenance, failure sensitivity and the logistics chain

This section is new in v7. Official LCA for BEVs, heat pumps, wind, solar PV and green H₂ treats the operational phase either in stylised form (assumptions of “average efficiency” and “average lifetime”) or not at all. In reality, every installation generates throughout its operating life: (a) service-transport CO₂, (b) maintenance emissions, (c) failure-related replacement emissions, and (d) supplier logistics for raw materials and spare parts. These items have been confirmed three times in primary literature.

What official LCA leaves out in the operational phase

  • Offshore-wind service vessels: MGO/HFO fuel over a 25-year operating life. Rarely included in “wind LCA 9–22 g/kWh”.
  • Wind-turbine blades 60–100 m long: escorted road transport — hundreds of thousands of kilometres of heavy haulage will be needed for 25,000 EU turbines by 2030.
  • Solar-PV inverter replacement every 10–15 years: new hardware production + installer kilometres. Not included in official solar-PV LCA calculations.
  • Out-of-warranty BEV battery replacement: 1–9% failure rate over 15 years; every replacement = 5,000 kg CO₂ for a new battery.
  • Marine freight for battery components from China/Korea to the EU: 4.1 kg CO₂e/kWh (T&E 2019, Kim et al.).
  • Heat-pump compressor leakage of R-410a (GWP 2088): one 2 kg leak = 4.2 t CO₂ equivalent.
  • Green-H₂ PGM catalyst replacement every 7–10 years: at 40 GW in the EU, ~1 Mt CO₂ per replacement cycle.

Onshore + offshore wind, confirmed three times

Duke University 2025: “vessels burning MDO or HFO for construction and O&M” — offshore-wind vessel emissions of 2.31–3.97 t CO₂e per GWh delivered. Complete O&M+installation: 8.43–40.42 t CO₂e/GWh. UK Government 2021 / Ørsted: 42 kt CO₂e for CTV+SOV in one year. Strathclyde 2015: 8.3 failures per offshore turbine per year.

Solar-PV inverter replacement

String-inverter lifetime: 10–15 years; replacement $1,500–3,500. Over 25 years: 1–2 replacements. CO₂ impact per string-inverter replacement ~50–100 kg of hardware + ~15 kg for the installer visit. For 100M EU solar-PV systems: 100M × 1.5 replacements × 75 kg = 11 Mt CO₂ extra over 25 years — currently absent from official solar LCA.

Heat-pump failure sensitivity

Green Reach Energy 2026 (UK RHI data): 2–4% failure rate per year; annual service £100–180. R-410a refrigerant leakage: with 100M EU heat pumps and 5% cumulative leakage over 20 years: 100M × 0.05 × 5 t = 25 Mt CO₂e from refrigerant emissions alone.

Turbine-blade transport

Wind-turbine blades are 60–100 m long and weigh 15–25 tonnes each. Texas charges a $420 permit fee for 90-metre blades with a police escort. North Dakota requires double escorts for blades over 75 m — $1,800–2,600 extra per trip. For 200 GW of EU offshore wind in 2050 (~17,000 turbines): cumulative blade-transport emissions of 2–5 Mt CO₂.

Part XI — The four alternatives assessed fairly

Solar panels — the segment where they work

Solar PV: 32–135 gCO₂/kWh full-LCA v7. For roofs and industrial, non-agricultural land: valid. On arable land: the worst choice (TOC −61%, crop loss 20–55%, 13–16% of which is globally linked to loss of arable land). Inverter replacement every 10–15 years.

Wind — LCA, system boundary, EOL and O&M

Wind: 16–55 gCO₂/kWh full-LCA v7 (formerly 7–22 official). Limitations: thermoset blades are unrecyclable; foundations account for 63% of EOL costs; NdFeB comes 90% through China; vessel O&M is 2.3–4 t CO₂e/GWh offshore; electricity only — no substitute for 75% of EU energy demand.

Electrification via heat pumps

New construction with perfect insulation on a green grid: valid. 80% of existing EU homes: COP collapse. Plus 2–4% failure/yr + R-410a leakage risk + copper bottleneck: 100M heat pumps × 15 kg Cu = 1.5 Mt.

Green hydrogen — materials + O&M bottleneck

Green H₂: 75–125 gCO₂/kWh full-LCA v7 (formerly ~50 official). Iridium: IRENA 3–7.5 GW/yr production against 100 GW/yr needed. EU 40 GW PEM in 2030 = 5.6 t Ir = 75% of global production. Catalyst replacement every 7–10 years = an additional 4 Mt CO₂ in the EU between 2030 and 2050. Well-to-wheel mobility efficiency: 45% vs. 80% for direct BEV.

Fair positioning

BEV is valid for urban, locally green applications. Heat pumps are valid for new construction with perfect insulation. Solar PV on roofs is valid. The Plant Route is the only solution for:

  • Aviation (battery + H₂ PGM impossible)
  • Long-distance shipping
  • Heavy freight
  • 80% of existing EU homes
  • Gigaton-scale permanent CO₂ sequestration
  • Full supply with a weak grid
  • Degraded land

The Plant Route avoids the materials bottleneck, grid reinforcement and operational complexity (O&M vessels, battery swaps, catalyst replacements and refrigerant leakage). SOFC is solid-state, Juncao is perennial, and there are no moving parts — a fundamentally different system architecture requiring less maintenance and fewer reserve materials.

Final conclusion

This report is built on one principle: gCO₂eq per functional unit, with every item included, including EOL, land impact, grid reinforcement, mining, materials bottlenecks and the operational phase (O&M, failures and logistics).

  • Transport: BEV v7 on the actual EU mix in 2025: ~128 gCO₂/km. Ethanol-SOFC: 39 g/km. Difference −70%.
  • Heating: Micro-CHP: 20–80 gCO₂/kWh for both heat and electricity. Net supplier. No moving parts.
  • Wind: 16–55 full-LCA v7 (formerly 7–22 official). +90–130% increase from O&M vessels, thermoset EOL, NdFeB and failures.
  • Solar PV: 32–135 full-LCA. Inverter replacement every 10–15 years.
  • Grid reinforcement + O&M: EU electrification: 2.66 Gt CO₂ of hidden embodied grid emissions; +800 Mt–1.5 Gt O&M.
  • Materials bottleneck: IEA/IRENA/DOE: electrification will encounter physical ceilings within 5–15 years.
  • Permanent sequestration: Root storage 37 t/ha/yr; BiCRS 95%/100 yrs; bioplastics.
  • System: The Plant Route delivers 33% more reduction and is €4.094 billion cheaper over 30 years. Physically feasible, with 3–4× lower O&M CO₂.

The choice is: which energy need receives which solution on the basis of a complete full LCA, including EOL, land impact, grid reinforcement, mining, materials bottlenecks and the operational phase with O&M and logistics? For aviation, shipping, heavy freight, existing homes, permanent CO₂ sequestration and degraded land, the Plant Route is not one option among many — it is the only route that physically works within the timescale required by the climate budget, without materials bottlenecks, hidden O&M emissions or unsustainable replacement chains. For the complete energy system, it is the skeleton to which the other routes attach wherever they fit.
