The Tale of Jacobus

In sixteen pages — in the tradition of Grimm

A fairy tale for children to hear, and for adults to recognise. Every page has two readings: on one side the tale in image and word, on the other the grown-up meaning.

Jacobus van Merksteijn · The Open Visor · Palma, 21 July 2026

Chapter 1

The rich kingdom

Once upon a time there was a continent that grew rich by making what the world needed. Mills turned. Ships sailed out. Fields stood full. In the harbour, goods changed hands.

And the people thought it would always stay this way.

The grown-up reading

The reconstruction period, 1945-1975.

For three decades, Western Europe was the workshop of the world. German machines, French chemistry, Italian fashion, Dutch harbours and dairy, British industry. What was built then is the well we have been drawing from ever since. Every pension reserve, every public service, every piece of cultural infrastructure stems from that period. Since 1975 we have been consuming this inheritance at an accelerating pace. The wealth seems to remain, because the buildings still stand. But the well itself is running dry.

Chapter 2

The storyteller

In a small room by the harbour sits an old man.

Before him on the table stands a glass sphere. In the sphere no lies can be seen, only what is real.

Come closer, he says, and look with me.

I tell only truths, and every one of them is fully documented.

The grown-up reading

The author's position.

This book is not a political pamphlet. It is a fairy tale, in the tradition of the Brothers Grimm — hard where reality is hard, but not cynical. Every figure named on the following pages can be verified against primary sources (Eurostat, the Bundesbank, INSEE, ONS, CBS). Every historical parallel is documented. The full evidence is set out in seven articles on openvizier.org. This book is their fairy-tale form — fit for children to hear and for adults to recognise.

Chapter 3

The mammoth tanker

Look into the sphere. A ship sails there. It is ten kilometres long. The bridge sits far at the back. On the bridge, they steer. Votes are cast, speeches are given, photographs are taken. But the bow sails ten kilometres ahead. And the bridge cannot see the bow. The bow is crumpling.

The grown-up reading

European governance in 2026.

The EU and its member states have become institutions so large and so complex that those governing them can no longer see operational reality. Regulation piles up without ever being revised. The GDPR, the 1992 Habitats Directive, MiFID II, Basel III — all still in force, in worlds that no longer exist. More rules, more agencies, more coordination. The bridge grows larger. The bow shrinks.

Chapter 4

More steam

The ship loses speed. The crumpled bow creates resistance. What does the bridge do? The bridge gives more steam. More coal on the fire. More pressure. More resistance. More steam. More resistance. And in the engine room they labour on, and no one asks whether the ship is still sailing anywhere at all.

The grown-up reading

Tax, red tape, subsidy.

When the economy falters, the answer is always the same: more tax, more red tape, more subsidy. More agencies to solve the problem that agencies often created in the first place. Regulatory burden on Dutch SMEs has doubled since 2005. The tax burden on labour reaches 52%. National debt has doubled since 2008. More engine power on the same crumpled bow.

Chapter 5

The citizens on the quay

On the quay stand the ordinary people. They see the bow from the side. They shout. They point. They try to warn. But the bridge is too far away. The wind does not carry their voices upward. And the crew has drawn the curtains, for inside it is brighter than out.

The grown-up reading

The elections that change nothing.

72% of the Dutch say they have little to no trust in the political system (SCP, 2025). Comparable figures hold in France, Germany, Italy. Yet policy barely shifts between coalitions. From right to left to right — the same EU rules, the same fiscal pressure, the same nitrogen crisis. The voter shouts from the quay. The bridge does not hear him, for the bridge hears only itself.

Chapter 6

The Land of Toys

But we, the people, have also become Pinocchio. For three generations we were led into the Land of Toys. Every wish was granted. No one asked anything of us. And unnoticed, we began to change. Our ears grew longer. Our tails grew. In the mirror, we do not see it.

The grown-up reading

The redistribution society and its price.

The post-war welfare state brought unprecedented prosperity and security. That is a historic blessing. But it has also raised three generations who no longer know how to repair a house themselves, start a business, carry a community, feed a family without an agency. More diplomas, less resilience. More rights, less capacity to act. We have been optimised for a system that will not survive.

Chapter 7

The crew of the bridge

In the great hall at the stern of the ship sit the governors. They are clever. They are well educated. They mean well. They sign papers. They pat each other on the shoulder. On the wall hangs a great map from the year 1992. No one looks through the window. Outside, it is storming.

The grown-up reading

The full broadside.

The current democratic leadership is not incompetent in the ordinary sense. It is systematically unfit in a way the system rewards. The debt has doubled. Nuclear power dismantled. Agriculture disrupted. Groningen left shaking. Industry watched as it departs. Above all: they did not prepare us. They offered us the Land of Toys and called it goodwill.

Chapter 8

Five ships, one reef

And it is not one ship. There are five. France sails in front. England follows. Then Italy and Germany together. Spain closes the line. Ahead of them a great reef rises in the mist. They are all sailing straight toward it. And on the bridge of every ship, they are voting on the colour of the lifeboats.

The grown-up reading

Five countries, five curves.

Political sociology recognises four phases of systemic crisis. Five major European countries now sit in phase 2. France goes through the trough first, between 2028 and 2031. The UK follows within a year. Italy and Germany arrive around 2033. Spain last. Historical precedents: Russia lost 44% of GDP over nine years, Argentina 20% over four years. We are inside that pattern, not outside it.

Chapter 9

The arena of shouting

In the great hall on land stands a platform. There, men and women shout at one another. Whoever cuts sharpest wins applause. Below, the crowd cheers as at a medieval joust. Above the door the clock strikes twelve. Nothing said there is truth. Only winning or losing is said.

The grown-up reading

The press as arena.

Talk-show culture is functionally identical to the Jacobin clubs of 1791-1794 — public spaces where the sharpest polemicist wins and shared diagnosis becomes impossible. Every major political upheaval of the last 250 years was preceded by precisely this state of the press: Weimar 1930, Russia 1917, France 1789. The press is no longer the watchman. The press is the arena.

Chapter 10

The thorns of thirty years

In the forest stands an old oak. From its branches hang old parchment scrolls. Around the oak grows a wall of thorns, thirty years old, dense and sharp. Behind the thorns lies a green valley with a working mill and a field of sun. Everyone can see the valley. No one can reach it.

The grown-up reading

Deciding without a view.

On a global scale, 21 gigatonnes of CO₂ per year can be captured on cost-neutral terms. More than half of global emissions. But we govern on the basis of laws from 1992 with no sunset clause. The Habitats Directive protects habitats that will not survive without climate adaptation anyway. Outdated regulation is not neutral. It is the hedge of thorns around every solution.

Chapter 11

The storyteller lays the cards

Come closer, says the old man. I lay the cards. On the first card, a ship. On the second, a puppet with donkey's ears. On the third, an arena. On the fourth, a hedge of thorns. On the fifth, a distant castle. The cards do not predict, he says. They order what is already certain. Look closely. And think about what you will do.

The grown-up reading

Ordering diagnosis into action.

The five cards summarise the five preceding chapters: the mammoth tanker (governance), Pinocchio (the citizen), the arena (the press), the hedge of thorns (regulation), the distant castle (the alternative). What is coming is not avoidable, but it is steerable. The crisis is not the problem. The crisis without a prepared alternative is the problem. Whoever has seen the cards can no longer say he did not know.

Chapter 12

The white horse departs

And then comes the day the white horse is saddled. On its back rides someone who can see ahead. In his bag he carries gold, a book, an instrument. Behind him the city begins to burn. He does not look back. What he carries is too important to lose in the fire.

The grown-up reading

Capital in temporary wintering.

Between 2026 and 2028, the European capital that can see clearly relocates to Switzerland, Singapore, the Balearics, the Gulf states. This is neither cowardice nor betrayal. It is preservation. What remains in phase 3 evaporates in phase 3. The capital returns once there is something to return to. Without capital preservation, there is no renaissance. This is how every historical recovery has worked: the Medici, Amsterdam, the Wirtschaftswunder, South Korea.

Chapter 13

The castle behind the thorns

Far beyond the hills lies a castle on a mountain. Around the castle grows a wall of thorns, thirty years old. In one tower, a light still burns. Within it sleeps the princess of the future. She is not weak. She waits. She waits for someone who can pass through the thorns.

The grown-up reading

The landing place exists.

The princess is Europe as it can be again: a productive core intact, democracy reformed, young people trained into people who once again know how to build. She does not sleep because there is no alternative. She sleeps because the thorns are still growing and the time has not yet come to wake her. The thorns are the laws of 1992, the institutions without a sunset clause, the habitat directives that no longer protect anything.

Chapter 14

The kiss that wakes the princess

Jacobus prunes the thorns. He clears a path. He finds the chamber. He bends over the sleeping princess and gives her the kiss. The kiss is Nova Democratia. The kiss is the For People Party. The kiss is Carbon Alert. The kiss is the cooperation with the countries on the equator. Through the window shines the first sun.

The grown-up reading

The four breadcrumbs.

Nova Democratia: a constitutional framework with sunset clauses on every law, performance placed above ideology. VMP: a political vehicle for the transition period — contesting elections until the existing order collapses. Carbon Alert: biomass injection plus the ethanol-bioplastic route, 21 gigatonnes per year, operational. Equator cooperation: 20,000 autonomous solar units on the equator bring the sun's energy into usable form via biomass.

Chapter 15

The princess awakens

And the princess awakens. She steps onto the balcony. She sees that the capital has returned — European, not foreign. She sees people arriving from every direction. She sees new ships, smaller and more agile than the old one. And the next chapter begins. That is the chapter you and your children will live. Or not. The choice is yours.

The grown-up reading

Whoever rides along wakes the princess.

Whoever sits in a corner and waits is not choosing neutrally. He is choosing the scenario in which authoritarian-populist parties or foreign capital determine the reconstruction. Whoever rides along with the white horse — with capital, knowledge, skill, will — gives the fairy tale its good ending. Whoever stays, burns with the ship. Whoever rides along, wakes the princess. There is no third option. The fairy tale ends however you let it end. Choose deliberately.
