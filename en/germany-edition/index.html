★ Germany edition

Where German soil gives European answers

Germany carries the structural burden of Europe's agricultural transition — from rapeseed cultivation between Berlin and the Baltic Sea to the CAP Strategic Plan that puts East German farmers under pressure. This edition looks for concrete, workable answers that make German farmers stronger rather than weaker — and position Germany as a European pioneer in protein sovereignty.

Lead article · June 2026

The Rapeseed Multiplier

285,000 hectares of winter rapeseed in Brandenburg and Mecklenburg-Vorpommern. Falling yields, falling direct payments, falling biofuel quotas. The Carbon-Alert architecture offers these farms a real multiplier factor of four to eight on farmer income — embedded in the CAP Strategic Plan 2023–2027, without subsidy fantasies.

For politicians and political decision-makers: This edition is not lobbying against the CAP Strategic Plan. It is a fulfilment of what the plan already demands — diverse crops, protein crops, extensification — on a single architecture.

For East German farmers: Here is an answer to the double-pressure scenario of falling rapeseed yields and falling CAP direct payments. On the same soil, with the same mechanisation, factor four to eight higher income.

For cooperatives and processors: This is your route to EUDR-compliant protein supply. Local production, no Brazilian soy, verifiable under East German conditions.
